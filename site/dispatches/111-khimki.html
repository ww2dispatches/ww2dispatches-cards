Battle Lines. Tuesday, 2 December 1941 · Khimki · Soviet Union

So close to Moscow, and no closer

Field Marshal Fedor von Bock told his generals the Red Army was close to breaking. By that evening he was admitting his own doubts.

2 December 1941. The day before, Bock had warned the high command that the belief in a Soviet collapse had proved "a phantasy." Now he told General Halder that "doubts of success are beginning to take definite form."

This is also the date often given for a German patrol reaching Khimki, 19 miles from the Kremlin, and seeing its spires. Much of that story traces to a 1963 bestseller by a former press chief of Hitler's Foreign Ministry. No solid source supports the spires.

The documented high water mark lay near Krasnaya Polyana, about twelve miles north of Moscow. Within days Bock's panzer groups were pulling back.
