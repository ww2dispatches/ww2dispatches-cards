Battle Lines. Friday, 3 December 1943 · Monte la Difensa · Italy

Up the cliffs of Difensa

Two sergeants went up a sheer rock face first, in the dark, and fixed the ropes for everyone behind them.

3 December 1943. Monte la Difensa, Hill 960, stood in the German Winter Line south of Cassino, looking down on the road to Rome. An American regiment had already failed to take it. Now it was the turn of the First Special Service Force, American and Canadian volunteers under Col. Robert T. Frederick, 36, trained in Montana for a Norway raid that never came.

The Force's 2nd Regiment climbed the northern face through rain, fog and snow. Sgt. Thomas Fenton and Sgt. Howard Van Ausdale led the way up the final cliff. The Forcemen reached the crest shortly before dawn and cleared the summit in about two hours. Fifth Army planners had expected two or three days.

The cost was heavy. In six days the Force lost 511 men, 73 of them killed.

At Anzio, Frederick later said, a German officer's diary called them the black devils. They preferred another name: the Devil's Brigade.
