Battle Lines. Friday, 24 November 1944 · Saipan · Mariana Islands

B-29s strike Tokyo from Saipan

The man in the copilot's seat of the first B-29 over Tokyo had already flown the Memphis Belle over Europe.

24 November 1944. At 06:15 Brig. Gen. Emmett O'Donnell Jr. lifted off from Isley Field on Saipan in Maj. Robert K. Morgan's own B-29, Dauntless Dotty, named for Morgan's wife, Dorothy. Morgan flew beside him, and 110 more bombers followed. It was the first raid on Tokyo since Doolittle in April 1942, aimed at the Nakajima engine plant at Musashino.

The mission had waited a week on wind and weather. The orders warned of 400 to 500 fighters. About 125 came up.

The worst trouble was higher. At 27,000 to 33,000 feet the bombers met the jet stream, and aiming became almost impossible. Seventeen turned back, and strike photos found just 16 bomb bursts in the target area.

A Japanese pilot drove his damaged fighter into the tail of one B-29, which went into the sea off Honshu with everyone aboard. By March 1945 Curtis LeMay had the bombers going in low, at night, with incendiaries.
