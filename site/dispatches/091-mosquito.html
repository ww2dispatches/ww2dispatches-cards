Blueprint. Monday, 25 November 1940 · Hatfield · Britain

The wooden bomber flies

Lord Beaverbrook called it "Freeman's Folly": a bomber with no guns at all, built almost entirely of wood.

25 November 1940. At 3:45 p.m. Geoffrey de Havilland Jr. took the yellow prototype up from Hatfield, with engine designer John E. Walker aboard, for about half an hour. It had been built in secret at Salisbury Hall and brought to the airfield by road.

The bet was that speed would protect it better than turrets. In trials in February 1941 it reached 392 mph at 22,000 feet, faster than the Spitfires of the day.

The folly became a bomber, night fighter, fighter bomber and reconnaissance plane, and 7,781 were built. De Havilland himself died in 1946 when his DH 108 jet broke up over the Thames Estuary.
