Battle Lines. Thursday, 9 November 1944 · Lorraine · France

Patton attacks in the rain

His corps commander asked him to wait out the rain. Patton told him to attack as scheduled or name his successor.

9 November 1944. Before dawn, the 90th Infantry Division crossed the swollen Moselle River at Koenigsmacker, north of Metz, and set up a secure bridgehead. It was the second day of Lt. Gen. George S. Patton's new offensive in Lorraine, aimed at Metz and the German border.

The Germans thought the weather was too bad for an attack, so it caught them by surprise. XII Corps had opened with the biggest artillery preparation in Third Army history: 540 guns fired 22,000 rounds.

Then the rain kept coming. Seven inches fell that November, about twice the normal amount, the region's worst flooding in 35 years. Mud and cloud cost the Americans much of their air advantage, and trench foot put almost as many men out of action as enemy fire.

Third Army closed the ring around Metz on 19 November. The Lorraine campaign cost it 55,182 combat casualties.
