Battle Lines. Wednesday, 29 November 1944 · Grosshau · Germany

Grosshau falls by moonlight

Just before noon an order came down to Col. Buck Lanham: take Grosshau today. He had planned to go around the village. Now his weakest battalion had to go straight at it.

29 November 1944. Lanham's 22nd Infantry had been fighting through the Hürtgen Forest since 16 November, and a first try at Grosshau had already failed. Lanham's plan was a wide march through the woods to seize the ridge behind it.

The head on attack went in within the hour. Within fifteen minutes the infantry was pinned down and both tanks had fallen to German assault guns. The official history calls it an abject failure.

Then the flanking force came through. At dusk one of Lanham's battalions reached the high ground northeast of the village, behind the defenders, and the tanks rolled into Grosshau firing. By the light of burning buildings and the first moon the division had seen in the forest, more than a hundred Germans surrendered.

Ernest Hemingway, reporting for Collier's, was with Lanham's regiment. Lanham became one of the models for the colonel in his novel Across the River and Into the Trees.
