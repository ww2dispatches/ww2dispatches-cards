Battle Lines. Monday, 2 November 1942 · El Alamein · Egypt

Supercharge breaks Rommel

"These are my regiments." The brigadier pointed to a handful of tanks. He had started the morning with 94.

2 November 1942. At 1:05 a.m., Montgomery's Eighth Army launched Operation Supercharge, its final push to break Rommel's line at El Alamein. British and New Zealand infantry moved off in the dark behind a creeping barrage from about 360 guns.

Then the 9th Armoured Brigade had to charge straight at a screen of German and Italian antitank guns, including deadly 88s. Brigadier John Currie warned he could lose half his strength. He was told Montgomery had "accepted the possibility of losing 100% casualties" to make the break.

The tanks went in at 6:15 a.m., with the rising sun behind them lighting them up for the gunners. Fewer than 25 were still running at the end, and 230 men were killed, wounded or missing. They smashed many guns but did not open the gap.

Rommel's counterattack failed, and the Afrika Korps lost about 70 tanks that day. That night he decided to fall back. Two days later his army was in retreat.
