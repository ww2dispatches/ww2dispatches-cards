Battle Lines. Friday, 5 December 1941 · Moscow · Soviet Union

Zhukov strikes back at Moscow

General Ivan Konev argued that he lacked the tanks and infantry to attack. On this morning his armies attacked anyway, across the frozen Volga.

5 December 1941. The Kalinin Front opened the Soviet counteroffensive before Moscow. In paralyzing cold the 29th Army crossed the Volga ice west of Kalinin and broke about a mile into the German line. Zhukov's Western Front joined in the next day.

A week earlier Stalin had handed Zhukov three reserve armies. Divisions from the Far East and Central Asia made it possible, and the spy Richard Sorge's reports that Japan would not attack helped Moscow risk stripping the east, though how much rested on him is still debated.

"If we do not do that in the next few days, it will be too late," General Aleksandr Vasilevsky had warned.

Klin fell on 15 December. On 19 December Hitler took command of the army himself. By early January the Germans had been pushed back 100 to 250 km from Moscow, and their hopes of a quick victory in the east were over.
