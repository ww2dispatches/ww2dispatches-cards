Battle Lines. Friday, 26 November 1943 · Off the Gilbert Islands · Pacific

Butch O'Hare's last flight

In the dark off the Gilberts, a Japanese bomber slipped in among three American planes. One of them was flown by Butch O'Hare, and it was never found.

26 November 1943. O'Hare, commander of the air group on USS Enterprise, was leading the first night fighter attack the Navy had ever launched from a carrier. Betty bombers were hunting the carriers off the Gilbert Islands, and two days earlier a submarine had blown up the escort carrier Liscome Bay with more than six hundred men.

His team was two Hellcats and one radar Avenger. Hazen Rand's radar found the bombers, and gunner Alvin Kernan set one on fire. When the Avenger switched on its running lights so the fighters could form up, a Betty crept in behind O'Hare.

Ensign Andy Skon saw O'Hare's lights go out as his Hellcat slanted down into the darkness.

For decades Kernan was blamed. A 1997 study concluded the Betty's nose gunner most likely fired the fatal shots.

O'Hare had already received the Medal of Honor, for taking on a formation of bombers alone to defend USS Lexington in 1942. In 1949 Chicago's Orchard Field became O'Hare International Airport.
