Battle Lines. Saturday, 4 December 1943 · Kwajalein · Marshall Islands

The second strike never flew

Someone took a pencil to Rear Admiral Charles Pownall's report and wrote two words beside his recommendations: "all defensive." Within two months another admiral had his carriers.

4 December 1943. Pownall's Task Force 50, six carriers with 386 aircraft, hit Kwajalein and Wotje in the Marshall Islands on the way home from the Gilberts. The strikes sank four cargo ships, damaged two old light cruisers and shot down 19 Japanese fighters. A photo plane found a new bomber strip on Kwajalein Island, about 70 percent finished.

A second wave was planned for noon, to wipe out the Japanese planes that were left. It never flew.

That night Japanese torpedo planes found the carriers. At 2333 a torpedo hit Lexington in the stern, killing nine men and knocking out her steering. Her crew rigged a hand operated emergency steering gear and brought her to Pearl Harbor.

Vice Admiral John Towers called Pownall "overcautious in plans and operations." In January the fast carriers sailed as Task Force 58 under Marc Mitscher. Pownall never led a combat command again.
