Battle Lines. Sunday, 22 November 1942 · Stalingrad · Soviet Union

Paulus asks to break out

At 7 p.m. General Friedrich Paulus sent two words up the chain: "Army surrounded." The reply took more than three hours.

22 November 1942. Soviet pincers were closing behind the Sixth Army at Stalingrad. Paulus flew into the pocket, set up at Gumrak airfield and asked for freedom of action, which meant a breakout to the southwest if the army could not hold. His corps commanders backed him, Walther von Seydlitz most loudly of all.

At 10.15 p.m. Hitler answered from his train: take up a hedgehog defence and await further orders. Two days later he decided the army would stay and be supplied by air.

Paulus surrendered on 31 January 1943, the morning after Hitler made him a field marshal.
