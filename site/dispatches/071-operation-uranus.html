Battle Lines. Thursday, 19 November 1942 · Serafimovich · Soviet Union

Operation Uranus begins

The code word was "Siren." Ten minutes after it came through, about 3,500 Soviet guns and mortars opened fire into the fog.

19 November 1942. The Red Army began Operation Uranus on the Don, northwest of Stalingrad. Fog and snow hid the targets, but the gunners had ranged in days earlier and the fire stayed accurate.

At 08:50 the 5th Tank and 21st Armies attacked the Romanian Third Army from the Serafimovich bridgehead and near Kletskaya. The Romanians had no antitank gun heavier than 47 mm. Their line broke by noon, and Soviet tank corps drove through the gaps.

That night Army Group B halted every German attack inside Stalingrad and pulled panzer divisions out of the city.

It had all been prepared in secret. Since the start of the month, Volga Flotilla boats had carried 160,000 troops and 430 tanks across the river at night.

On 23 November the pincers met near Sovetsky, east of Kalach, closing a ring around the German Sixth Army and part of the Fourth Panzer Army, between 250,000 and 300,000 Axis troops.
