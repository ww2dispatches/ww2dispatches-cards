Battle Lines. Tuesday, 14 November 1944 · Koenigsmacker · France

Patton crosses the Moselle

Five days earlier, the river here had been almost a mile wide in flood, and his men had crossed it in assault boats under the guns of a fort.

14 November 1944. Lt. Gen. George S. Patton went across the Moselle to see the bridgehead his 90th Infantry Division had won at Koenigsmacker, north of Metz. With Maj. Gen. Walton Walker and Brig. Gen. James Van Fleet, he rode in an armored car to the division command post, then toured the freed town in jeeps.

The crossing had begun at 3:30 a.m. on 9 November. Fort Koenigsmacker had four 100 mm guns in revolving armored turrets, and its fire wrecked bridging equipment and harried the men in the boats.

Engineers blasting their way into the fort used so much explosive that more had to be dropped in by air. Taking it cost about 111 American casualties.

Patton called it "an epic river crossing." Five days later, Third Army closed the ring around Metz.
