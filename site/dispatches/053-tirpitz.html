Battle Lines. Sunday, 12 November 1944 · Tromso · Norway

The Tirpitz goes down

Each bomber carried a single bomb weighing 12,000 pounds. Britain had been trying to sink this one ship for years.

12 November 1944. RAF Lancasters of No. 9 and No. 617 Squadrons, led by Wing Commander J. B. Tait, attacked the German battleship Tirpitz near Tromso, in the far north of Norway. Each carried a Tallboy, a deep penetration bomb.

The bombers began their run at 09:35. At least two Tallboys hit her directly. Within minutes she was listing hard, and after a magazine exploded she rolled over and capsized.

Somewhere between about 950 and 1,204 German sailors died. Men trapped inside the upturned hull were cut free through her exposed bottom; 82 were rescued that way.

Tirpitz carried eight 15 inch guns and had hardly fought. But simply by existing she forced the Royal Navy to keep powerful forces at home to watch her. Midget submarines, carrier dive bombers, and an earlier Tallboy raid had all tried to finish her.

With Tirpitz gone, the last German battleship threat to the Arctic convoys was over.
