Battle Lines. Monday, 27 November 1944 · Isley Field, Saipan · Mariana Islands

Japan strikes back at the B-29s

Out of ammunition, a Japanese pilot landed his Zero on an American airfield and kept fighting with what he had left. Brig. Gen. Haywood Hansell watched it happen.

27 November 1944. Three days after the first B-29 raid on Tokyo from the Marianas, the Imperial Japanese Navy struck back at their base on Saipan. Two Betty bombers from Iwo Jima came in low before dawn, destroying one Superfortress and damaging eleven.

At noon Zero fighters of the 252nd Air Group arrived, each carrying a bomb, and destroyed three more B-29s. The last pilot opened fire with his pistol until infantry rifle fire killed him.

After that day Admiral Nimitz ordered a heavy strike on Iwo Jima, and the bombing of the island went on until the Marines landed there.
