Battle Lines. Saturday, 18 November 1944 · Metz · France

Patton's men break into Metz

Time magazine noted that Metz had never once been taken by storm. Attila destroyed it in 451, but nobody was defending it then.

18 November 1944. Troops of the US Third Army closed on the fortress city in Lorraine. Men of the 5th Infantry Division's 10th and 11th Infantry crossed the city limits from the south, while the 95th Infantry Division pressed in from the north and west. Overhead, P-47 Thunderbolts dive bombed the city.

It came at a price. Before dawn the Germans destroyed the bridges over the Moselle west of Metz, and one exploded beneath a patrol of the 378th Infantry. Eight men on it were killed.

The next morning the two divisions linked up inside the ring. The German field army had pulled back and left the garrison behind, and most of the Germans outside the forts put up only token resistance before surrendering. French resistance fighters helped the Americans.

Fighting in the city ended on 22 November, closing four years of German annexation. The outer forts were left to starve; the last held out until 13 December.
