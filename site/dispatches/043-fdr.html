Statecraft. Tuesday, 7 November 1944 · Washington · D.C.

Roosevelt wins a fourth term

He is the only president ever elected four times. Less than three months after his last inauguration, he was dead.

7 November 1944. In the middle of the war, Franklin D. Roosevelt beat New York governor Thomas E. Dewey, 432 electoral votes to 99. It was the closest of his four presidential elections, about 53.4 percent to 45.9.

His new running mate was Senator Harry S. Truman of Missouri, chosen after party leaders turned against Vice President Henry A. Wallace. Roosevelt's serious health problems were kept from the public. To answer the whispers, he toured New York City for four hours in an open car in pouring rain.

Millions of service members voted by absentee ballot. The next day in France, Signal Corps cameras filmed soldiers parading behind a jeep with a sign reading "Roosevelt Elected." The Army's own notes called their show of joy "very stagey."

He died on 12 April 1945, and Truman became president. The 22nd Amendment now limits presidents to two elected terms.
