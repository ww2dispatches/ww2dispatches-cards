Battle Lines. Sunday, 5 November 1944 · Manila Bay · Philippines

Carriers strike Manila Bay

Admiral Shima was ashore at a staff conference when the planes came. He watched from land as his flagship was blown apart.

5 November 1944. Two weeks after the Battle of Leyte Gulf, American carrier planes struck Japanese airfields on Luzon and shipping in Manila Bay. The aim was to wreck the air power feeding planes, including kamikazes, into the fight for Leyte.

The heavy cruiser Nachi, Vice Adm. Kiyohide Shima's flagship at Surigao Strait, tried to flee the bay at high speed. Planes from Essex hit her first, and planes from Lexington finished her that afternoon with bombs, rockets and torpedoes. She broke into three pieces near Corregidor. About 807 of her crew were killed.

That afternoon a burning Japanese plane carrying a bomb crashed into Lexington's island. About 50 of her men were killed, the worst single loss in her long career.

A Navy cameraman aboard the new carrier Ticonderoga filmed the day's strike. His slate reads "Manila, 11/5/44."
