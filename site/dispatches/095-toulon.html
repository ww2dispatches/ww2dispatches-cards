Battle Lines. Friday, 27 November 1942 · Toulon · France

France sinks its own fleet

A German officer reached the battleship Strasbourg and demanded that Admiral de Laborde hand her over. The admiral told him he was too late.

27 November 1942. German combat groups drove into Toulon at 4 a.m. to seize the French fleet in Operation Lila, as Germany took over the rest of Vichy France. Laborde, flying his flag in Strasbourg, was no friend of the Allies. He had refused Admiral Darlan's call to join them and had even proposed sailing out to attack them.

But standing orders since 11 November said the ships must be scuttled rather than fall into foreign hands. German tanks rolled through the main gate of the naval base at 5:25, and the signal went out to the ships: "Scuttle! Scuttle! Scuttle!"

Crews opened the sea valves and set off demolition charges. About 77 vessels were destroyed, among them 3 battleships, 7 cruisers and 15 destroyers. Twelve Frenchmen were killed.

Hitler's prize was gone, and the scuttled ships never fought for either side.

Laborde paid later. In 1947 he was sentenced to death for treason, partly for not taking the fleet over to the Allies. The sentence was commuted, and he was pardoned in 1951.
