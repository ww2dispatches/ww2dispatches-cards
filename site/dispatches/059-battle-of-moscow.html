Battle Lines. Saturday, 15 November 1941 · Moscow · Soviet Union

Moscow braces for the blow

For weeks the German drive on Moscow had been stuck in the autumn mud. Then the frost came, the ground set hard, and the panzers could move again.

15 November 1941. Army Group Centre renewed Operation Typhoon. The German Ninth Army struck first, south of Kalinin. The next day the 3rd and 4th Panzer Groups joined in, swinging north of the capital toward Klin and Solnechnogorsk. On 18 November Guderian's Second Panzer Army pushed off below Tula.

Moscow had been under a state of siege since 20 October. Somewhere between 250,000 and 600,000 Muscovites, most of them women, had dug antitank ditches and trenches around the city, and its workers had formed 12 militia divisions.

General Georgy Zhukov's Western Front stood in the way, reinforced in early November by about 100,000 men, 300 tanks and 2,000 guns. Rokossovsky's 16th Army gave ground slowly on the Volokolamsk road.

Klin fell on 23 November, and German tanks crossed the Moscow Volga Canal at Yakhroma. Then the cold deepened, the attack ran out of strength, and in early December the Red Army struck back.
