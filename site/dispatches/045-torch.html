Battle Lines. Sunday, 8 November 1942 · Casablanca · French Morocco

Torch lands in North Africa

About 107,000 Allied troops headed for the beaches in the dark, and nobody knew for certain whether the French defenders would shoot.

8 November 1942. Operation Torch began. American and British troops landed in French North Africa, around Casablanca in French Morocco and at Oran and Algiers in Algeria.

Patton's Western Task Force, about 35,000 Americans, had sailed straight across the Atlantic from Hampton Roads, Virginia. The forces of Vichy France did fight, hardest of all around Casablanca, where the unfinished battleship Jean Bart traded fire with USS Massachusetts.

It was the U.S. Army's first large scale amphibious landing under fire. Landing craft grounded on sandbars nobody had scouted, and vehicles failed in the surf. The landing boat Patton meant to take ashore was damaged by the blast of his own cruiser's 8 inch guns, and most of his luggage went overboard.

Most of the fighting was over within three days. Casablanca surrendered on 11 November. Torch opened the road to Tunisia, and its lessons shaped the landings in Italy, France, and the Pacific.
