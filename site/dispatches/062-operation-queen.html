Battle Lines. Thursday, 16 November 1944 · Eschweiler · Germany

Operation Queen

The planners drew the bomb line two miles out in front of their own men. They remembered the bombs that fell short in Normandy.

16 November 1944. The Allies opened Operation Queen, by the US Army's own count the heaviest air strike of the war flown in direct support of troops on the ground. About 1,200 Eighth Air Force heavy bombers dropped 4,120 tons of fragmentation bombs around Eschweiler, Weisweiler and Langerwehe, meant to hit troops while leaving the roads intact for the American advance.

RAF Bomber Command struck Duren, Julich and Heinsberg behind the front. Then the artillery opened up, and at 12:45 the infantry of the US First and Ninth Armies attacked toward the Roer River.

The bombing disappointed. Most forward German positions survived. But one German division was arriving by train as the bombs fell, and an artillery battalion caught at the Julich railroad station was all but wiped out.

The infantry paid for every yard. Some rifle companies of the 16th Infantry lost more than half their men in two days in the woods.
