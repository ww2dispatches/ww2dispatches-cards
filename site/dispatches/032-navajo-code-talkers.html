Battle Lines. Monday, 1 November 1943 · Bougainville · Solomon Islands

Navajo voices on Bougainville

The night after the landing, a radio message stumped radiomen for three hours. A Navajo Marine cracked it in under five minutes.

1 November 1943. Navajo code talkers came ashore with the 3rd Marine Division at Bougainville. Pvt. Bill Toledo saw his first combat that day. He had memorized all 211 terms of a code built from his own language.

A submarine was besh lo, "iron fish." America was "Our Mother." Even Navajo speakers who were not trained talkers could not follow it, because the code sat inside the language.

Here, Cpl. Henry Bake Jr. and Pfc. George H. Kirk run a radio in a jungle clearing on Bougainville, December 1943. The program stayed secret until 1968.
