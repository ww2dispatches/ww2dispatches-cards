Home Front. Thursday, 4 November 1943 · Tule Lake · California

The Army takes over Tule Lake

At 9:50 p.m., troops with tanks and jeeps mounted with machine guns rolled into a camp holding thousands of Japanese Americans.

4 November 1943. Tule Lake in northern California had become the segregation center for people labeled "disloyal" after a government loyalty questionnaire.

After a farm truck overturned and killed a worker, the farm workers went on strike. That evening, people saw trucks leaving the warehouses and believed camp food was going to strikebreakers. Scuffles broke out, and the camp director called in the Army.

Men seized that night were held in a tent stockade, a prison inside the prison. Martial law lasted until 15 January 1944. Tule Lake was the last camp to close, on 20 March 1946.
