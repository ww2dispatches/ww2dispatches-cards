Statecraft. Saturday, 29 November 1941 · Berlin · Germany

Todt tells Hitler: no victory

Hitler's own armaments minister walked into the Chancellery and told him the war could no longer be won by arms.

29 November 1941. Fritz Todt brought the industrialist Walter Rohland, just back from the Eastern Front and shaken by the Soviet tanks he had seen there. According to Rohland's memoir, Todt said: "This war can no longer be won by military means." Hitler answered that a negotiated peace had almost no chance. No transcript survives.

Twelve days later Hitler declared war on the United States, the country whose factories Todt had warned about.

In February 1942, the morning after another heated argument with Hitler, Todt's plane crashed just after takeoff from Rastenburg. An inquiry ruled out sabotage.
