Blueprint. Sunday, 2 November 1947 · Long Beach Harbor · California

The Spruce Goose flies once

Howard Hughes told the U.S. Senate that if his giant plane was a failure, he would probably leave the country and never come back. Three months later he flew it.

2 November 1947. With copilot David Grant beside him, Hughes lifted the H-4 Hercules off Long Beach Harbor and flew it low over the water for less than a minute. It was the only flight the plane ever made.

It was born in 1942 as a way to carry troops and cargo over the ocean, safe from U-boats. Wartime limits on metal meant it was built mostly of laminated birch, so even the nickname "Spruce Goose" was wrong.

Hughes never flew it again, but he kept it in a climate controlled hangar until his death in 1976.
