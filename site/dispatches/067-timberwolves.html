Battle Lines. Friday, 17 November 1944 · Stolberg · Germany

Timberwolves at Stolberg

Maj. Gen. Terry Allen drilled his division in something most commanders avoided: attacking in the dark. Omar Bradley later called it "the only division I know of that was prepared for night combat."

17 November 1944. Allen's 104th Infantry Division, the Timberwolves, pressed its attack northeast of Stolberg, Germany. The prize was the Donnerberg, Hill 287, guarded by mines, wire and three giant pillboxes, with German observers on top. The attack went in at noon behind fighter bombers and a detailed artillery plan, and still made little headway.

Around noon the next day the defense cracked. That night one company slipped almost 1,000 yards through the dark Eschweiler woods to seize a road junction.
