Blueprint. Wednesday, 2 December 1942 · Chicago · United States

Fermi's pile comes alive

Norman Hilberry stood beside the world's first nuclear reactor holding an axe. If anything went wrong, he would cut the rope.

2 December 1942. In a disused squash court under the stands of Stagg Field in Chicago, Enrico Fermi had the control rods drawn out step by step. At 3:25 p.m. he announced that the reaction was self sustaining, the first controlled nuclear chain reaction. It made about half a watt, enough for a small light bulb.

Forty nine people were present. Eugene Wigner poured Chianti into paper cups, and the signatures on the bottle's label are the only record of who was there. Arthur Compton phoned the news in code: "The Italian navigator has landed in the New World."
