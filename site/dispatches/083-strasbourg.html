Battle Lines. Thursday, 23 November 1944 · Strasbourg · France

The flag over Strasbourg

A French soldier named Maurice Lebrun volunteered to climb 142 meters up a cathedral spire with a homemade flag. Why that spire? Because of an oath sworn at a desert fort more than three years before.

23 November 1944. General Philippe Leclerc's French 2nd Armored Division drove on Strasbourg at dawn, guided by Alsatian resistance fighters. The city's ring of forts held up most of the columns. Lt. Col. Rouvillois's men slipped in through Schiltigheim, surprised the defenders and reached the cathedral with their tanks. His radio message was a code: "Tissu est dans iode," tissue is in iodine. They were inside.

Early that afternoon Lebrun, a spahi of the division, made the climb and fixed the flag to the spire.

The oath went back to March 1941. At Kufra in Libya, after his men crossed the Sahara and took the Italian fort, Leclerc had them swear to keep fighting until "our colours, our beautiful colours, fly over the cathedral of Strasbourg."

The Germans still destroyed the Rhine bridge to Kehl before the French could take it. And in January 1945, when Eisenhower weighed leaving the city undefended, de Gaulle called it a national disaster. Strasbourg was held.
