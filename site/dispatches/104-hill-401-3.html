Battle Lines. Thursday, 30 November 1944 · Grosshau · Germany

Half a battalion for one hill

Lt. Col. William Burton Jr. led his men up a bare hill under German guns. By nightfall they held the top, and half of them were gone.

30 November 1944. A day after Grosshau fell, Col. Buck Lanham turned his 22nd Infantry toward Gey and Düren. He sent the 46th Armored Infantry Battalion, newly attached from the 5th Armored Division, up Hill 401.3, the open height that commanded the whole Grosshau clearing.

German fire swept the slopes from morning until dark. The battalion took the hilltop at nightfall, through what the official history calls sheer determination. Burton received the Distinguished Service Cross, as did Staff Sergeants Robert Henley and Brady Kelley.

Beside the hill, Lanham's 2nd Battalion fought all day just to reach its starting line. Its two attacking companies had fewer than a hundred men between them.

On 1 December the regiment finally came out of the Hürtgen Forest. Its 18 days there cost about 85 percent of a regiment of some 3,250 men. The division commander reported that it had been milked of all offensive ability.
