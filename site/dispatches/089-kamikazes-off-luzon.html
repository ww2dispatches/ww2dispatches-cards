Battle Lines. Saturday, 25 November 1944 · Off Luzon · Philippines

Kamikazes hit four carriers

Lt. Donald DiMarzo was USS Intrepid's fire marshal. The day the kamikazes found his ship, he led the fire parties into the flames and did not come back.

25 November 1944. Task Force 38 was launching strikes on Luzon from about 160 miles east of Manila when about a dozen Japanese planes came in low and fast past the fighter patrol. Radar was crowded with American planes coming home, and the 5 inch guns could not pick out the attackers in time.

At 12:50 a Jill torpedo plane crashed onto Intrepid's flight deck. Eight minutes later a second plane hit amidships. Fires trapped sailors in smoke filled compartments, and 69 of her men died.

DiMarzo organized the fight on the hangar deck through the flames and explosions and was killed there. His Navy Cross citation says he helped save his ship. Her fires were out within two hours, and she stayed on station.

Essex and Cabot were hit the same afternoon, and burning wreckage fell on Hancock. Halsey wrote that Intrepid "went through hell." The task force pulled back to Ulithi to rest and rethink its defenses.
