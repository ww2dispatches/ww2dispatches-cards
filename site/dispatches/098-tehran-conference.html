Statecraft. Sunday, 28 November 1943 · Tehran · Iran

The Big Three meet at Tehran

At 9:30 that morning Stalin sent Roosevelt a warning: Tehran was full of Axis sympathizers. By 3 p.m. the President was living inside the Soviet compound.

28 November 1943. The Tehran Conference, code named Eureka, opened that day. Averell Harriman told Roosevelt that if he kept refusing Soviet quarters, the Americans would answer for any harm to Stalin crossing the city. Roosevelt gave in. To keep up appearances, some of his staff went back to the American Legation after settling him in.

At 3:15 p.m. Stalin called on him, their first meeting. "I am glad to see you," Roosevelt said. "I have tried for a long time to bring this about."

At the first plenary an hour later, Roosevelt, the youngest of the three, welcomed his elders. Stalin told them Italy was no road to the heart of Germany; the way in was through northern France.

Before the conference closed, the British and Americans committed to Overlord by May 1944, and the Soviets promised a major offensive in the east to hold German troops away from it.
