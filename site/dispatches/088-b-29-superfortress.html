Blueprint. Friday, 24 November 1944 · Saipan · Mariana Islands

The bomber built for Japan

Gen. Henry H. Arnold was so sure of this bomber that the Army ordered 1,500 of them before the prototype had flown.

24 November 1944. 111 B-29s took off from Saipan for Tokyo, the first raid on the Japanese capital from the Marianas. This was the range the plane was built for. It came from a 1940 call for a bomber that could fly 400 mph and travel 5,000 miles, and Boeing's design first flew in September 1942.

Four 2,200 horsepower Wright R-3350 engines gave it 357 mph. In December 1943 American leaders chose to keep it out of Europe and turn it on Japan.

3,970 were built. Two of them dropped the atomic bombs in August 1945.
