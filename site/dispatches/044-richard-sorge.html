Statecraft. Tuesday, 7 November 1944 · Sugamo Prison · Tokyo

Stalin's spy in Tokyo hanged

He was a Nazi Party member with an office inside the German embassy in Tokyo. He was also Stalin's spy.

7 November 1944. Japan hanged Richard Sorge at Sugamo Prison in Tokyo. The trap fell at 10:20 a.m., on the anniversary of the Russian Revolution. His collaborator Hotsumi Ozaki was hanged earlier the same day.

Working under cover as a German journalist, Sorge warned Moscow in 1941 that Germany was about to attack. Stalin ignored him. That autumn he reported that Japan would strike south, not at the Soviet Union. Historians still debate how much that shaped Stalin's move of troops from Siberia to defend Moscow.

Twenty years after his death, the Soviet Union named him a Hero of the Soviet Union.
