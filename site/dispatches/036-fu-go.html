Blueprint. Friday, 3 November 1944 · Honshu · Japan

Fu-Go balloon bomb

Japanese schoolgirls glued it together, working long shifts without being told what they were building. It was a weapon meant to cross an ocean on its own.

3 November 1944 brought the first launch of the Fu-Go, Japan's balloon bomb. The envelope was washi paper from the bark of the kozo bush, four to five thin layers glued with konnyaku paste, made from a starchy root also eaten as food.

A metal ring of sandbags hung below. Barometers fired small charges to drop a sandbag whenever the balloon sank too low, and a valve vented gas if it rose too high. When the ballast ran out, the ring let go of the bombs.

U.S. geologists studied the sand in captured ballast bags and traced it to beaches in Japan.
