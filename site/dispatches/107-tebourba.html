Battle Lines. Tuesday, 1 December 1942 · Tebourba · Tunisia

Last stand at Tebourba

Major Herbert Le Patourel went up the hill alone with a pistol and grenades. The army announced his Victoria Cross as a posthumous award. He was still alive.

1 December 1942. At 7.45 a.m. the 10th Panzer Division struck the Allied spearhead east of Tebourba, less than three weeks after the Torch landings. In its path stood the 2nd Battalion, The Hampshire Regiment, just two days in the line. The U.S. Army's official history says their stubborn resistance held the attackers far short of their objective.

They held for three days. On 3 December, when the Germans took a high feature on the flank, Le Patourel, 26, led four volunteers up under very heavy fire. When all of them had been killed or wounded, he went on by himself and did not come back.

That night the survivors slipped away along a track by the river in small groups. The battalion had lost 495 men, about 72 percent of its strength. Tebourba fell on 4 December.

A correction in the next week's London Gazette deleted the word posthumous. Le Patourel was a badly wounded prisoner, and he received his Cross in Cairo.
