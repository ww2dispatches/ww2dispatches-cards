Battle Lines. Saturday, 22 November 1941 · Lake Ladoga · Soviet Union

The Road of Life opens

Major V. A. Porchunov led 60 trucks onto ice that was still too thin to trust. Behind him was a city where the bread ration for everyone but workers had fallen to 125 grams a day.

22 November 1941. Leningrad had been cut off by land since September. Its one link to the rest of the Soviet Union was Lake Ladoga, now frozen. Porchunov's column set out across it that day, the first motor convoy on what was called Military Road No. 101. People came to know it as the Road of Life.

Most of his trucks were GAZ AA polutorkas, light trucks rated at a ton and a half. By the most common account, the first run came back with 33 tons of flour.

The ice kept giving way. Many drivers rode standing on the running board, steering with the door hanging open so they could jump if the truck broke through. About 40 trucks went under in the first week.

It was worth the risk. Hunger and cold were killing about 100,000 people a month by early 1942. On 25 December, with food coming across the lake, the ration went up for the first time. By April the ice road had carried more than 350,000 tons into the city, and on the way back it carried out more than half a million civilians.
