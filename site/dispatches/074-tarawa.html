Battle Lines. Saturday, 20 November 1943 · Betio, Tarawa Atoll · Gilbert Islands

The Marines land on Tarawa

PFC Edward J. Moore reached for his rifle and found it shot in half. His amtrac, the first one ashore, hung almost on end against a five foot seawall.

20 November 1943. The 2nd Marine Division under Maj. Gen. Julian C. Smith landed on Betio, the main island of Tarawa Atoll in the Gilberts. Betio was about 2 miles long, never more than about 10 feet above the sea, and the Japanese had made it a fortress.

The first amtracs reached Red Beach One at 9:10 a.m. But a neap tide left only about 3 feet of water over the coral reef. The Higgins boats needed about 4, so they grounded, and the Marines in them waded 500 to 1,000 yards to the beach under fire.

The fight for the island lasted about 76 hours. More than 1,000 Marines and Navy corpsmen were killed and more than 2,000 wounded.

Headlines about "The Bloody Beaches of Tarawa" shocked Americans. Its lessons, among them more and better armored amtracs and heavier, more accurate bombardment, shaped every Pacific landing that followed.
