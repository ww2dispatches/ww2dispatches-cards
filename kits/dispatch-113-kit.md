## YouTube Shorts description
Of 925 guns firing that evening, 346 aimed at one mountain. Then about 600 men climbed its cliffs in the dark with their weapons.

The First Special Service Force was a single unit of American and Canadian volunteers under one chain of command, activated in July 1942 and trained in Montana for winter raids in Norway that never happened. By late 1943 the Fifth Army was stuck at the German Winter Line south of Cassino. Monte la Difensa, Hill 960, looked down on the road to Rome, and an American regiment had already failed to take it in November. At 4.30 p.m. on 2 December 1943 the guns opened up. Of 925 artillery pieces firing in Operation Raincoat, 346 aimed at Difensa alone and fired more than 22,000 rounds in an hour. At dusk the Force's 2nd Regiment, about 600 men, started up the steep northern face for a surprise attack, through rain, fog and snow. The Force's commander was Col. Robert T. Frederick, 36. The last 500 feet were sheer cliff. Scouts Sgt. Thomas Fenton and Sgt. Howard Van Ausdale climbed ahead and anchored ropes, and the men hauled themselves up in the dark with their weapons. Some men slipped on loose gravel and the Germans fired flares. The Forcemen reached the crest shortly before dawn on 3 December and cleared the summit in about two hours. Fifth Army planners had expected it to take two or three days. The cost was heavy. Lt. Col. Thomas MacWilliam, commanding the regiment's 1st Battalion, was killed, as was Capt. William Rothlin, one of his company commanders. Then came three days of rain, sleet and freezing cold on the bare summit, with every load of food and ammunition carried up by hand. On 4 December Frederick signalled: "Every man in unit exhausted." In six days the Force lost 511 men: 73 dead, 9 missing, 313 wounded or injured and 116 evacuated for exposure and exhaustion. The 142nd Infantry relieved them on 8 December. Frederick went on to lead the Force through Anzio and into Rome, and by the end of the war he had earned eight Purple Hearts. At Anzio, Frederick later said, a dead German officer's diary called them the black devils, who came all around and were never heard. The Forcemen, who blackened their faces for night raids, preferred another name: the Devil's Brigade. On 5 December 1944, almost a year to the day after Difensa, the Force paraded for the last time as its Canadians left. Its battle honours live on in the Canadian Special Operations Regiment and the U.S. Special Forces.

This is U.S. Army Signal Corps film from a reel titled 1st Special Service Forces, shot in Italy's mountains in the winter of 1943 to 1944: a heavy howitzer crew loading and firing from a sandbagged pit in a mountain valley. The reel is undated, the gun may belong to another unit, and it is not film of the night of the climb. The original film is silent.
