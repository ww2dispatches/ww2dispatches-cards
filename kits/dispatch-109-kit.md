## YouTube Shorts description
That rocket launcher is going under the wing of a Piper Cub, a 65 horsepower plane built to spot targets for the artillery.

The L-4 was the Army's version of Piper's civilian J-3 Cub, a light two seat high wing monoplane with a 65 horsepower Continental engine. Piper supplied Cubs for the Second Army's maneuvers in Tennessee in June 1941. Its main job was to be a flying observation post for the artillery, spotting targets and adjusting fire. It also served as a liaison plane and an air ambulance. Three flew from the carrier USS Ranger on 9 November 1942, during the Operation Torch landings in North Africa, spotting for naval gunfire. The main production run, from the first O-59s to the L-4J, came to 5,549 aircraft. Pilots began to arm them. Maj. Charles Carpenter, a high school history teacher from Illinois before the war, flew an L-4 scouting for Patton's 4th Armored Division and bolted bazookas to its wing struts, eventually six, three under each wing. He named the plane Rosie the Rocketer. Diving low, he fired on German tanks and armored cars in Lorraine in the autumn of 1944. Accounts differ on his exact score and dates. By late September 1944 the AP's Wes Gallagher was already calling the former teacher a legend in Patton's army. The idea spread. In autumn 1944 Ninth Air Force Service Command armament specialists in France were photographed fitting three bazookas under each wing of a Cub, and another Army photo shows a bazooka armed Cub in flight. On 1 December 1944 Signal Corps cameramen filmed men of the 104th Infantry Division Artillery placing a rocket launcher under the wing of an L-4. The plane took off, circled the field and fired the launcher. Carpenter's plane, L-4H serial 43-30426, survived the war, ended up in an aviation museum in Graz, Austria, and was restored to flying condition for the Collings Foundation.

This is U.S. Army Signal Corps film of men of the 104th Infantry Division Artillery fitting a rocket launcher under the wing of an L-4 on 1 December 1944, then flying it. The firing in the air is too small to see in the film, so it is not in this clip. Major Carpenter's own plane is not in the film. The original film is silent.
