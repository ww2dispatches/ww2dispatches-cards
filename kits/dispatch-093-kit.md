Steered by Joystick: The Glide Bomb That Hit LST-282 #shorts

## YouTube Shorts description
A crewman steered this kind of bomb by joystick all the way to its target. This is what one hit did to USS LST-282.

The Henschel Hs 293 was a guided glide bomb: a small winged missile dropped from a bomber, boosted by a liquid fuel rocket that fired for about 10 seconds, then gliding onto a ship. A crewman in the bomber steered it all the way with a joystick, sending commands over a radio link (the FuG 203 transmitter) while he watched flares or a lamp on its tail. It was designed at Henschel by a team under Herbert Wagner, and it weighed about 1,045 kg. Its warhead was built from a 500 kg bomb. On 26 November 1943, about 16:30, Luftwaffe He 177 bombers with Ju 88 escorts attacked convoy KMF 26 off Bougie, Algeria. The He 177s launched a wave of Hs 293s. Between about 17:15 and 17:25, the He 177 flown by Hans Dochtermann released a glide bomb that struck the port side of HMT Rohna, a British India Steam Navigation troopship carrying American soldiers. Rohna was the only ship lost. 1,138 men died, 1,015 of them Americans, the largest loss of U.S. troops at sea from enemy action in a single incident. The loss was kept quiet. In February 1944 Washington admitted only that more than 1,000 men had died on an unnamed troopship, the ship was not named until 1945, and a fuller account came out only in 1967. The same weapon sank USS LST-282 off southern France on the evening of 15 August 1944, the first day of the landings there. Army cameramen filmed her burning that night.

This is U.S. Army Signal Corps film of USS LST-282 burning off southern France on the night of 15 August 1944, after a hit by the same weapon. It is not film of the Rohna. The original film is silent.
