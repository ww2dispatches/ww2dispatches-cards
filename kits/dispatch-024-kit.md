## YouTube Shorts description
On 28 October 1942, the Army declared the pioneer road of the Alaska Highway finished: more than 1,500 miles from Dawson Creek, British Columbia, to Delta Junction, Alaska, cut through wilderness in about eight months. Three days earlier, near Beaver Creek, Corporal Refines Sims Jr. of the 97th Engineers, one of three African American regiments on the project, met Private Alfred Jalufka of the 18th Engineers as their crews joined.

This is real U.S. Army Signal Corps film of the Alcan Highway in the Yukon, shot in 1942 (the exact day is not recorded): trucks on the new road, a bulldozer at work on a new section, and a convoy in the mountains. The original film is silent.

Film: U.S. Army Signal Corps, 111-ADC-1806, U.S. National Archives (catalog.archives.gov/id/15613), public domain.
Sources: The Alaskan Canadian Highway, U.S. Army; Soldier's Summit, Alaska Magazine.

## Source film
US ARMY CAMP IN ROCKY MOUNTAIN REGION, CANADA, sometime in 1942. U.S. Army Signal Corps film 111-ADC-1806 (NARA 15613), silent. Shot card: the Alcan Highway east of Kluane Lake, Yukon, a truck convoy on the highway, and road machinery at work on a new section. Army trucks pass on the new road, a bulldozer works a new section, and a convoy winds through the mountains. The exact filming date is not recorded, so the caption says 1942, not 28 October. U.S. government work, public domain (NARA's blanket 'possibly copyright' flag on Signal Corps reels). Clip uses 4:44.5 to 4:50.0, 7:46.6 to 7:51.6, and 5:00.0 to 5:05.0. The Alaska Highway compilation 111-ADC-9672 was not used because NARA notes possible copyright.
