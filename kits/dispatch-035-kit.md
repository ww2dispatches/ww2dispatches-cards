## YouTube Shorts title
Japan's Paper Balloon Bombs, First Launched 3 November 1944 #shorts

## YouTube Shorts description
Before dawn on 3 November 1944, the Imperial Japanese Army launched its first Fu-Go balloon bombs toward North America. Each balloon was made of kozo mulberry paper and filled with hydrogen, and the winter jet stream was meant to carry it more than 5,000 miles across the Pacific in a few days. A ring of sandbags kept it aloft at night, and when the sandbags were gone the same ring dropped the bombs. Japan launched more than 9,000; only about 300 were found or seen in North America. On 5 May 1945, one killed Elsie Mitchell and five children near Bly, Oregon, the only people killed by enemy action in the continental United States during the war.

This is real U.S. Army film of a captured balloon, dated 1946 by the National Archives: a balloon in flight, the film's own map of its route, and the ring that carried the ballast and bombs. It does not show the 1944 launch. The original film is silent.

Film: U.S. Army Signal Corps, 111-ADC-9990, U.S. National Archives (catalog.archives.gov/id/23754), public domain.
Sources: Japan's World War II Balloon Bomb Attacks on North America, Robert C. Mikesh, Smithsonian Annals of Flight No. 9; Smithsonian Magazine; Atomic Heritage Foundation.
