## YouTube Shorts description
On 16 November 1944 the Allies launched Operation Queen, which the US Army's official history calls the largest air attack of the war in direct support of ground troops. About 1,200 Eighth Air Force heavy bombers dropped 4,120 tons of fragmentation bombs around Eschweiler, Weisweiler and Langerwehe, chosen to hit troops without cratering the roads, while RAF Bomber Command struck Duren, Julich and Heinsberg behind the front. Then the guns joined in: on the First Army front alone, 694 guns fired an hour long preparation, helped by tank guns, mortars and a battalion of 4.5 inch rocket launchers. At 12:45 the infantry of the US First and Ninth Armies attacked toward the Roer River. The bombing disappointed, with most forward German positions surviving, and the 1st Infantry Division's 16th Infantry had to fight through the woods toward Hamich under heavy shelling.

This is real U.S. Army Signal Corps film shot on 16 November 1944: rocket launcher crews of the 18th Field Artillery, VII Corps, near Mausbach, loading and firing 4.5 inch rockets. The original film is silent.

Film: U.S. Army Signal Corps, 111-ADC-2715, U.S. National Archives (catalog.archives.gov/id/16519), public domain.
