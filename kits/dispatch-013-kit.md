## Film notes
From Desert Victory (1943), the British Ministry of Information documentary built on Army Film and Photographic Unit footage. Crown copyright expired, public domain (PD-UKGov on Wikimedia Commons). The night gun flashes are genuine footage of the barrage; the cameramen slowed their clockwork cameras so the flashes would not white out. The film's staged Pinewood close ups of soldiers are not used.

Sound: the film's own soundtrack under these shots, cut with the picture: gun thunder, no music or narration. It was mixed for the 1943 film, so it may be studio or field effects rather than sound recorded that night. Loudness normalized to about -16 LUFS. Clip: 29:21.27 to 29:24.0, then 27:07.36 to 27:20.2 of the Commons copy.

## YouTube Shorts title
The Night 900 Guns Fired at Once: El Alamein, 1942

## YouTube Shorts description
At 9:40 p.m. on 23 October 1942, nearly 900 British and Commonwealth guns opened fire at once along the line at El Alamein in Egypt. It was the start of Montgomery's offensive, and the beginning of the end for Rommel's army in North Africa. Twelve days later the Afrika Korps was in retreat.

This is real film of the barrage, shot at night by British Army cameramen, who slowed their cameras so the flashes would not burn out the picture. The sound is the original soundtrack from the 1943 film.

Film: Desert Victory (1943), British Ministry of Information, Army Film and Photographic Unit footage, public domain (Crown copyright expired), via Wikimedia Commons: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Desert_Victory_(1943).webm
Sources: Second Battle of El Alamein, Wikipedia; "Making Desert Victory," Forces News.
