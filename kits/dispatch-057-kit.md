## YouTube Shorts description
On 14 November 1944, Lt. Gen. George S. Patton went across the Moselle to see the bridgehead his 90th Infantry Division had won at Koenigsmacker, north of Metz. Five days earlier, early on 9 November, the 90th had crossed the Moselle in assault boats at the height of a flood. Opposite Fort Koenigsmacker the river was almost a mile wide. The fort's guns wrecked bridging equipment and harried the men in the boats, and the water ran so high and fast that five days passed before armor could cross in force. Patton called it "an epic river crossing." Third Army closed the ring around Metz on 19 November.

This is real U.S. Army Signal Corps film shot on 14 November 1944: Patton with Maj. Gen. Walton Walker and Brig. Gen. James Van Fleet, the armored car in the muddy street, and jeeps in the freed town. The original film is silent.

Film: U.S. Army Signal Corps, 111-ADC-2688, U.S. National Archives (catalog.archives.gov/id/16492), public domain.
