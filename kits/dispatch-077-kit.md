## YouTube Shorts description
On 21 November 1945, the second day of the Nuremberg trial, Robert H. Jackson, chief of counsel for the United States, opened the prosecution's case before the International Military Tribunal. Jackson was a sitting justice of the US Supreme Court on leave, appointed by President Truman. Before he spoke, every defendant present pleaded not guilty; Hermann Goering tried to make a statement and was cut off. Jackson told the court: "The wrongs which we seek to condemn and punish have been so calculated, so malignant, and so devastating, that civilization cannot tolerate their being ignored, because it cannot survive their being repeated." He called the Allies' decision to try their captive enemies "one of the most significant tributes that Power has ever paid to Reason." The trial ran until 1 October 1946.

This is real U.S. Army film from the courtroom on 21 November 1945. The sound is the original recording of Jackson's voice from the source film.
