## YouTube Shorts description
On 3 November 1942, Hitler ordered Rommel to stand fast at El Alamein, to yield not a yard of ground, and to show his troops no other road than that to victory or death. Rommel wrote that the order demanded the impossible, but he obeyed and ordered his men to hold. On the morning of 4 November the British broke through, and by early afternoon, Rommel wrote, enemy armour had burst a 19 kilometre hole in his front. By his own figures the Germans were down to 36 tanks, from 249 at the start of the battle. That same day the Afrika Korps commander, General Wilhelm Ritter von Thoma, was captured. At 17:30 Rommel ordered a general retreat without waiting for Hitler's reply. Alamein was the first great British land victory over the German army.

This is real film from the end of the battle, shot by the British Army Film and Photographic Unit and used in the 1943 documentary Desert Victory: British tanks pushing through the smoke and heading west. The film does not date each shot, so these may not be from 4 November itself. The sound is the original soundtrack of the source film.

Film: Desert Victory (1943), British Ministry of Information, Army Film and Photographic Unit footage. Crown copyright expired, public domain (Wikimedia Commons).
Sources: Erwin Rommel on his Defeat in Africa, German History in Documents and Images; The End of the Beginning, National Army Museum; The Bright Gleam of Victory, International Churchill Society; Second Battle of El Alamein, Wikipedia.

## Source film
Desert Victory (1943), British Ministry of Information documentary with film shot by the Army Film and Photographic Unit; Crown copyright expired, public domain (PD-UKGov on Wikimedia Commons; 480p copy from Brian's Source Film folder). From the film's breakthrough section, which its narrator introduces at 44:37 to 44:53 ('the entire weight of their armour was in readiness... it sailed through the gap. Its purpose was simple, to destroy Rommel's armour'); the narrator is silent from 44:53 to 48:12, apart from a shouted 'Fire!' in the scene at 47:24, which is left out. Clip uses 45:49.65 to 45:53.25 (Grant tanks charging through smoke as a shell bursts), 47:18.80 to 47:21.35 (a tank racing forward in its own dust), 45:44.45 to 45:45.55 (two Shermans crossing the desert), 45:20.60 to 45:26.00 (a line of tanks rolling forward) and 45:40.20 to 45:41.90 (a tank passing toward the horizon). The film does not date each shot, so the caption says it shows the end of the battle. Left out: the shots of German officers with binoculars at 45:28 to 45:33 (captured enemy newsreel) and any shot under the narration. Sound: the film's own soundtrack under these shots (engines, shell bursts and gunfire; no narration by speech recognition, no music by spectrogram), cut with the picture and normalized to -16 LUFS.
