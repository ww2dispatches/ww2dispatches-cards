## YouTube Shorts description
On 11 November 1944, Armistice Day, Winston Churchill and General Charles de Gaulle rode together up the Champs-Elysees in Paris, less than three months after the city was freed from German occupation. Some members of Churchill's cabinet had tried to talk him out of going, since German stragglers were thought to be hiding in the city. Parisians packed the sidewalks, windows, and rooftops, chanting "Vive de Gaulle! Vive Churchill!" At the Arc de Triomphe, the two men laid a wreath at the Tomb of the Unknown Soldier and stood at the salute through a minute of silence, then walked through the crowd to watch an hour long parade.

This is real U.S. Army Signal Corps film of the day: the leaders' cars arriving with a motorcycle escort, the crowds and flags, the salute at the Arc, and the party walking through the crowd. The original film is silent.

Film: U.S. Army Signal Corps, 111-ADC-2661, U.S. National Archives (catalog.archives.gov/id/16465), public domain.
Sources: Churchill in Paris, Armistice Day 1944, The Churchill Project, Hillsdale College; Wild with joy, New Zealand Herald, 13 November 1944, Papers Past, National Library of New Zealand; Provisional Government of the French Republic, Wikipedia.
