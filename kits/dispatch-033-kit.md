## YouTube Shorts description
At 1:05 a.m. on 2 November 1942, the British Eighth Army launched Operation Supercharge, its final push to break Rommel's line at El Alamein. The infantry moved off in the dark behind a creeping barrage from about 360 guns. At 6:15 a.m. the 9th Armoured Brigade charged straight at a screen of German and Italian antitank guns, with the rising sun behind the tanks lighting them up for the enemy gunners. Its commander, Brigadier John Currie, had been told Montgomery accepted the possibility of losing 100% casualties to make the break. The brigade began with 94 tanks; fewer than 25 were still running at the end, and 230 of its men were killed, wounded or missing. It did not open the gap, but Rommel's counterattack that day failed, and the Afrika Korps lost about 70 tanks.

This is real film from the final days of the battle, shot by the British Army Film and Photographic Unit and used in the 1943 documentary Desert Victory: infantry under shellfire and the dust of the fighting. The film does not date each shot, so these may not be from 2 November itself. The sound is the original soundtrack of the source film.

Film: Desert Victory (1943), British Ministry of Information, Army Film and Photographic Unit footage. Crown copyright expired, public domain (Wikimedia Commons).
Sources: Operation Supercharge, NZHistory (Ministry for Culture and Heritage); Second Battle of El Alamein, Wikipedia (citing Playfair and Barr); John Cecil Currie, Wikipedia.

## Source film
Desert Victory (1943), British Ministry of Information documentary with film shot by the Army Film and Photographic Unit; Crown copyright expired, public domain (PD-UKGov on Wikimedia Commons; 480p copy from Brian's Source Film folder). From the film's Supercharge section, which its narrator introduces at 42:21 ('the blow we meant to be mortal was struck'); the narrator is silent from 42:45 to 44:07. Clip uses 43:57.10 to 44:04.50 (infantry running through a huge shell burst), 43:11.50 to 43:17.20 (infantry pinned down as shells land, with a close shot of a Bren gunner), and 43:48.10 to 43:51.10 (a vehicle in the dust of a shell burst). The film does not date each shot, so the caption says it shows the final days of the battle. Left out: the tank shot at 42:46 to 42:55 (the film's score plays under it) and the shots of German vehicles at 43:25 to 43:30 and 43:44 (possible captured enemy newsreel). Sound: the film's own soundtrack under these shots (shell bursts and gunfire; no narration by speech recognition, no music by spectrogram), cut with the picture and normalized to -16 LUFS.
