19 Guns Against the Panzers: Outpost Snipe, El Alamein, 1942 #shorts

## YouTube Shorts description
On 27 October 1942, four days into the Second Battle of El Alamein, the 2nd Battalion of the Rifle Brigade and a battery of Royal Artillery gunners held a desert position codenamed Snipe with nineteen six pounder antitank guns. All day, German and Italian tanks attacked them. Their commander, Lieutenant Colonel Victor Turner, joined one gun crew as loader and kept going with a head wound until the last tank was driven off. A month later a committee counted between 52 and 57 enemy armored vehicles knocked out around Snipe. Turner received the Victoria Cross.

This is real film of the fighting at El Alamein in October 1942, from the British documentary Desert Victory: a shell bursting as infantry advance, guns firing, and a tank charging through the dust. The film does not show Snipe itself. The sound is the original soundtrack of the 1943 film.

Film: Desert Victory (1943), British Ministry of Information, Army Film and Photographic Unit footage, public domain (Crown copyright expired), via Wikimedia Commons: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Desert_Victory_(1943).webm
Sources: Defence of Outpost Snipe, Wikipedia; Victor Buller Turner VC citation, London Gazette 20 November 1942; "Outpost Snipe," Warfare History Network.
