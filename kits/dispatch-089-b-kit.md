## YouTube Shorts description
In faded Navy color, a carrier burns while a plane circles overhead. On Intrepid alone, two crashes killed 69 men.

Around noon on 25 November 1944, Task Force 38's carriers were launching strikes on Luzon from about 160 miles east of Manila when about a dozen Japanese planes slipped past the fighter patrol, coming in low and fast. Radar screens were cluttered with American planes coming home to land, so the 5 inch guns could not pick up the diving attackers in time. At 12:50 a Jill torpedo plane crashed onto USS Intrepid's flight deck. At 12:58 a second plane crashed into her flight deck amidships. The two hits killed 69 of Intrepid's men and set serious fires that trapped sailors in smoke filled compartments. Her fire marshal, Lt. Donald DiMarzo, organized and led the fire parties on the hangar deck through the flames and explosions. He was killed fighting the fires and was awarded the Navy Cross after his death; his citation says he helped save his ship. Intrepid stayed on station, and her fires were out within two hours. USS Essex was hit on the port edge of her flight deck by a plane flown by Lt. Yoshinori Yamaguchi. It landed among aircraft fueled for takeoff, killing 15 and wounding 44. Halsey wrote that Intrepid "went through hell". The strain forced Task Force 38 back to Ulithi to rest and rethink its defenses, and Intrepid left for repairs on 26 November, reaching San Francisco on 20 December.

This is U.S. Navy color film of the daylight attack on Task Group 38.2, with Intrepid and Cabot, on 25 November 1944. The reel does not record which carrier appears in each shot. The original film is silent.
