## YouTube Shorts description
Its gunners aimed turrets they were not sitting in, through an analog computer. Watch these giants come home.

The B-29 began with a 1940 Army Air Corps demand for a bomber that could fly 400 mph and travel 5,000 miles. Boeing's answer, the Model 345, first flew on 21 September 1942. Gen. Henry H. Arnold believed in it so strongly that the Army ordered 1,500 before the first prototype had flown. It was the first bomber with pressurized crew areas. Its guns sat in remote controlled turrets. An analog computer fire control system let gunners at sighting stations aim turrets they were not sitting in. Four 2,200 horsepower Wright R-3350 radial engines gave it a top speed of 357 mph, with a wingspan of 141 feet 3 inches. In December 1943 American leaders decided not to send it to Europe but to use its range against Japan. The first B-29 raids on Japan came from China on 15 June 1944. From March 1945 Curtis LeMay sent them in low, at night, with incendiaries. On 6 and 9 August 1945 B-29s dropped the atomic bombs on Hiroshima and Nagasaki. In all, 3,970 were built.

This is U.S. Army Air Forces film of B-29s landing on Saipan on 4 March 1945, returning from a mission, months after the first raid from the Marianas. The original film is silent.
