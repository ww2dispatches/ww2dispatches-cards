## YouTube Shorts description
On 13 November 1944, carrier planes of Admiral William Halsey's Task Force 38 struck Japanese shipping in and around Manila Bay in the Philippines. More than 350 carrier planes went after the light cruiser Kiso as she got under way in the bay. She had orders to leave for Brunei that evening, carrying Vice Admiral Kiyohide Shima and his staff. Three bombs hit her, and she lost power and settled into the shallow water west of Cavite that afternoon. Other Japanese destroyers and transports were also sunk in and around the bay that day, among them the destroyer Okinami.

This is real U.S. Navy film shot by a cameraman flying with the strike: Avenger torpedo bombers in close formation, ships in the bay, and columns of smoke rising over the Manila waterfront. The original film is silent.

Film: U.S. Navy, Strike on Manila Bay Area, 428-NPC-6221, U.S. National Archives (catalog.archives.gov/id/77801), public domain.
Sources: Japanese cruiser Kiso, Wikipedia; Kiso, Pacific Wrecks; Japanese destroyer Akishimo, Wikipedia.
