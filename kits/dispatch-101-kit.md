## YouTube Shorts title
The Charge Stalled. The Flank Won: Grosshau, 1944 #shorts

## YouTube Shorts description
Their charge across the open fields stalled within minutes. Then a flanking battalion came out on the ridge behind the village as night fell.

Since 16 November 1944 the US 4th Infantry Division had been fighting east through the Hürtgen Forest toward Düren and the Roer River. Col. Charles T. "Buck" Lanham's 22nd Infantry was aimed at Grosshau, a village in a clearing on the forest's eastern edge. A first try on 25 November failed. Only three tanks and a tank destroyer got out of the woods with the infantry, German antitank gunners in Grosshau picked off the tanks, and artillery drove the men back into the forest. In two more days of limited fighting the 22nd Infantry suffered over 250 casualties, and two of its companies had fewer than fifty men each in the line. For 29 November Lanham planned a wide flanking march through the woods to the north to seize the ridge behind Grosshau, hoping the Germans in the village would then give up without another charge across open fields. German shelling delayed the start. Just before noon the division chief of staff, Col. Richard S. Marr, stepped in, in the division commander's name, and directed that Grosshau be taken that day. That meant a head on assault. Lanham's only free battalion was Major Blazzard's 2nd Battalion, as weak as any in the division. Within an hour one company with two tanks and a tank destroyer attacked down the road from the west. Within fifteen minutes the infantry was pinned in the open and both tanks had fallen to German assault guns. Eight more tanks tried to swing around to the southwest. Two hit mines at once, and the rest could not get out of the woods through the minefields and bog. The sun was going down on what the official history calls an abject failure. Then Lanham's flanking force cut the Grosshau to Gey highway, and as night came one battalion emerged on the ridge northeast of the village, behind the defenders. Tanks and tank destroyers used the dusk to reach the stalled infantry and rolled into Grosshau firing. Resistance collapsed in minutes. By the light of burning buildings and a moon, the first moon the division had seen since entering the forest, the infantry mopped up. More than a hundred Germans surrendered. Ernest Hemingway, covering the war for Collier's, was with Lanham's regiment. The two had met in Normandy, and Hemingway followed him into the Hürtgen, reaching the regimental command post on 15 November and seldom leaving his side. Hemingway later drew on the Hürtgen for his novel Across the River and Into the Trees. Lanham was one of the models for its hero, Colonel Cantwell.

This is U.S. Army Signal Corps film of 4.5 inch rocket batteries in the Hürtgen Forest: smoke hanging over the truck mounted launchers, crews at the racks, rockets laid out on the ground, and a launcher truck moving to a new position. The slate chalked on the film reads 29 November 1944 and the shot card says 30 November; either way it is the forest on the day Grosshau fell or the day after, not the village itself. The original film is silent.
