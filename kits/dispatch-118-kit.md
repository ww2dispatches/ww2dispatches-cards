## YouTube Shorts description
A Hellcat slams onto a carrier deck and bursts into flame. Grumman had built it to take punishment.

The Hellcat began as an improved version of Grumman's F4F Wildcat and grew into an entirely new fighter, built to beat the Japanese Zero. The prototype first flew on 26 June 1942. Production started in October 1942, before the prototype had finished testing. The Navy accepted the first production F6F-3 in January 1943. It was built to take punishment: heavy cockpit armor, a bullet resistant windshield, self sealing fuel tanks and an armored oil tank, pulled along by a 2,000 horsepower Pratt and Whitney R-2800 engine. On 10 November 1943, Ensign Byron M. Johnson of Fighting Squadron Two brought F6F-3 Number 30 aboard USS Enterprise, then on her way to the Gilberts. The fighter slammed into the carrier's port 20mm gun gallery and its ruptured belly tank caught fire. The crash jammed the canopy and Johnson could not get out. Lieutenant Walter L. Chewning Jr., the catapult officer, ran over, climbed up the side of the burning plane, forced the canopy open and pulled him out. Johnson escaped without significant injury. A Navy photographer caught Chewning in the act, and the picture became one of the best known photographs of the war. Three and a half weeks later, on 4 December 1943, Enterprise and five other carriers of Task Force 50 sent 246 planes against Kwajalein and Wotje in the Marshall Islands. Their fighters shot down 19 Japanese fighters. The Hellcat's best day came on 19 June 1944 over the Marianas, when Japan lost more than 270 aircraft to 26 Hellcats lost. It came to be called the Great Marianas Turkey Shoot. Grumman built 12,275 Hellcats before production ended in November 1945. Hellcats were credited with a victory to loss ratio of 19 to 1.

This is U.S. Navy color film from the carrier strikes on the Gilbert and Marshall Islands in late 1943: a Hellcat bursting into flame on landing, another crash landing as the deck crew run to it, and Avengers in formation. It is not dated to 4 December, and the burning fighter is not identified; it is not Ensign Johnson's crash. The original film is silent.
