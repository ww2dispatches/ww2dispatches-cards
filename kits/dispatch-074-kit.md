## YouTube Shorts description
On the morning of 20 November 1943 the 2nd Marine Division under Maj. Gen. Julian C. Smith landed on Betio, the main island of Tarawa Atoll in the Gilbert Islands. Betio was about 2 miles long and nowhere more than about 10 feet above the sea, and the Japanese had turned it into a fortress. A neap tide left only about 3 feet of water over the reef, so Higgins boats grounded and their Marines had to wade 500 to 1,000 yards to shore under fire. The tracked amtracs could crawl over the reef; of 125 used at Betio, only 35 were left at the end. Organized resistance ended on 23 November. More than 1,000 Marines and Navy corpsmen were killed and more than 2,000 wounded, and 4,690 of the island's defenders died. The lessons shaped every Pacific landing that followed.

This is real U.S. Navy color film shot on 20 November 1943, the camera original behind With the Marines at Tarawa: the bombardment of Betio and a wave of landing craft heading in. The original film is silent.
