## YouTube Shorts description
In mid November 1941, with the autumn mud frozen hard, the German army renewed its drive on Moscow. The Ninth Army attacked south of Kalinin on 15 November, two panzer groups followed the next day, and Guderian struck below Tula on the 18th. Rokossovsky's 16th Army gave ground slowly on the Volokolamsk road while Muscovites, most of them women, dug ditches and trenches around the capital. Klin fell on 23 November, and by the 28th German spearheads stood less than 20 miles from the Kremlin. Then the attack ran out of strength in deep cold, and in early December the Red Army struck back.

This film shows Soviet riflemen charging with bayonets, a cavalry charge across the snow, and infantry advancing, from a 1942 Soviet documentary of the battle. The shots are not dated to 15 November. The film's narration and score are left off, so the clip is silent.

Film: Defeat of the German Armies near Moscow (Central Studio of Documentary Film, 1942), via Wikimedia Commons, public domain.
