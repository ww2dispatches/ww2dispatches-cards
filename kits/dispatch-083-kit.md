Three Columns Stopped, One Got Through: Strasbourg, 1944 #shorts

## YouTube Shorts description
Three French columns were stopped at the forts. Another came in by a route nobody expected and drove its tanks to the cathedral.

At Kufra Leclerc made his men swear an oath, dated 2 March 1941: "Swear not to lay down your arms until our colours, our beautiful colours, fly over the cathedral of Strasbourg." By November 1944 Leclerc commanded the French 2nd Armored Division, fighting under the U.S. XV Corps of Gen. Wade Haislip, which broke through the Saverne Gap in the Vosges on 22 November. At dawn on 23 November the division drove on Strasbourg in several columns, guided by Alsatian resistance fighters. The aim was the Rhine and the bridge to Kehl. By mid morning three of the columns were stopped by the city's ring of forts, linked by ditches and antitank defences. The column of Lt. Col. Rouvillois, coming in by an unexpected route through Schiltigheim, broke through, caught the defenders by surprise and drove its tanks through the city to the cathedral. Early that afternoon a spahi of the division, Maurice Lebrun, volunteered to climb the cathedral's 142 m spire and fix an improvised French flag to it. The oath of Kufra was kept. The Germans blew the Rhine bridge to Kehl before the French could take it.

This is U.S. Army Signal Corps film of the entry into Strasbourg, November 1944: tanks rolling down a tram street past civilians, the Strassburg-Schiltigheim town sign, a Sherman carrying the French 2nd Armored Division's map of France insignia, a captured Adolf Hitler Strasse sign hung on a tank as a trophy, and the cathedral spire above a damaged street. The film is not dated to the day and runs silent.

Film: U.S. Army Signal Corps, 111-ADC-9629, U.S. National Archives (catalog.archives.gov/id/23393), public domain.
Sources: Liberation and defence of Strasbourg, Chemins de memoire, French Ministry of the Armed Forces (French); Liberation of Strasbourg, Wikipedia; Capture of Kufra, Wikipedia (citing the Ordre de la Liberation); General LeClerc, U.S. Army Signal Corps photo 111-SC-192467, National Archives.
