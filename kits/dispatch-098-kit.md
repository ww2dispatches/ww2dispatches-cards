## YouTube Shorts description
Stalin, Roosevelt and Churchill, together for the first time. Before they left, the invasion of France was set for May 1944.

The Tehran Conference, code named Eureka, ran from 28 November to 1 December 1943. It was the first time Franklin D. Roosevelt, Winston Churchill and Joseph Stalin met together. At 9:30 a.m. on 28 November, Stalin sent word through Ambassador Averell Harriman that he was worried about the distance between the American Legation, where Roosevelt was staying, and the Soviet Embassy, because Tehran was full of Axis sympathizers. Harriman told the President that if he kept refusing quarters in the Soviet compound, the Americans would be responsible for any harm to Stalin driving across the city. Tehran had been under complete German control only months before. Roosevelt accepted the Soviet invitation. At 3:00 p.m. Roosevelt, Admiral William Leahy, Harry Hopkins and Major John Boettiger drove to the Soviet Embassy to live there as guests of the Soviet government. Stalin lived in a smaller house in the same compound, and the British Legation was a block away. At 3:15 p.m. Stalin called on Roosevelt, their first meeting ever. The President greeted him: "I am glad to see you. I have tried for a long time to bring this about." The first plenary meeting opened at 4:00 p.m. in the Soviet Embassy. Roosevelt, as the youngest of the three, said he ventured to welcome his elders. Generals George Marshall and Henry Arnold missed that first meeting. It had been called at very short notice, and both were out on an auto tour of Tehran. The payoff: before the conference ended, the British and Americans committed to Operation Overlord, the invasion of northern France, by May 1944, and the Soviets agreed to a major offensive in the east to hold German troops away from it.

This is film from the Tehran Conference: Stalin, Roosevelt and Churchill seated on the portico of the Soviet Embassy with their staffs, photographers at work in front. The portico sitting is generally dated 29 November, the day after the opening. The original film is silent.

Film: Cairo and Teheran Conferences, November to December 1943, FDR Library Motion Pictures 71-8:62, U.S. National Archives (catalog.archives.gov/id/124450702).
