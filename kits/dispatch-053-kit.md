## YouTube Shorts description
On the morning of 12 November 1944, RAF Lancasters of No. 9 and No. 617 Squadrons, led by Wing Commander J. B. Tait, attacked the German battleship Tirpitz near Tromso, in the far north of Norway. Each bomber carried a single Tallboy, a 12,000 pound deep penetration bomb. They scored at least two direct hits and more near misses. The run began at 09:35. Within minutes the ship was listing hard, and after a magazine exploded she rolled over and capsized. Somewhere between about 950 and 1,204 German sailors died; 82 men trapped inside the upturned hull were cut free through her bottom.

Tirpitz had hardly fought, but simply by existing she forced the Royal Navy to keep powerful forces in home waters. Britain had tried for years to sink her: midget submarines in September 1943, carrier dive bombers in April 1944, and Lancasters with Tallboys in September 1944. With Tirpitz gone, the last German battleship threat to the Arctic convoys and the North Atlantic was over.

This is real film of the raid, shot by an Australian film crew flying with No. 463 Squadron RAAF: Tallboys bursting around the ship as she lies under the smoke. The original film is silent.

Film: Operation Catechism, No. 463 Squadron RAAF, Australian War Memorial C189542. Australian Crown copyright expired, public domain (Wikimedia Commons).
