## YouTube Shorts description
On 30 October 1944, in the forests of the Vosges Mountains in France, the 442nd Regimental Combat Team broke through to the 1st Battalion of the 141st Infantry, cut off by German troops since 24 October. The 442nd was a segregated unit of Nisei, the American born sons of Japanese immigrants. 211 men of the Lost Battalion walked out. By most counts the 442nd took more casualties in the rescue than the number of men it saved.

This is real U.S. Army Signal Corps film shot in the Vosges on 30 and 31 October 1944: Nisei soldiers moving up through the woods, then the tired men of the 1st Battalion walking out. The original film is silent.

Film: U.S. Army Signal Corps, 111-ADC-3423, U.S. National Archives (catalog.archives.gov/id/17226), public domain.
Sources: Lost Battalion (Europe, World War II), Wikipedia; Rescue of the Lost Battalion, Go For Broke National Education Center.
