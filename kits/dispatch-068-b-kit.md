## YouTube Shorts description
On 19 November 1944 the 377th and 378th Infantry and the 95th Division's Task Force Bacon all entered Metz proper, and the two divisions linked up inside the ring of forts that morning. The German field army had pulled back east and left the garrison to its fate, so there was no real house to house fight: most Germans outside the forts fired a few shots, then came over with their hands up. French resistance fighters of the FFI and two French intelligence networks helped the Americans. Fighting in the city formally ended on 22 November, though the last outer fort held out until 13 December. For France, it ended four years of German annexation.

This is real U.S. Army Signal Corps film from the 'Inside Metz' story, shot on 19 November 1944, the day after this dispatch's date: infantry on the streets and German prisoners under FFI guard. The original film is silent.

Film: U.S. Army Signal Corps, 111-ADC-2717, U.S. National Archives (catalog.archives.gov/id/16521), public domain.
