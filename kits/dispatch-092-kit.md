## YouTube Shorts description
Look for F-13 on the side. That Wildcat belonged to Butch O'Hare, the Navy's first ace of the war.

On the night of 26 November 1943, Lieutenant Commander Edward "Butch" O'Hare, commander of the air group on USS Enterprise, led the U.S. Navy's first night fighter attack ever launched from an aircraft carrier. O'Hare's team was two Hellcat fighters and one radar equipped Avenger torpedo plane: O'Hare and Ensign Warren "Andy" Skon in the Hellcats, and Lieutenant Commander John Phillips flying the Avenger, with Hazen Rand on the radar and Alvin Kernan in the rear turret. O'Hare took off at about 6 p.m. in F6F Hellcat Bureau No. 66168. Rand's radar led them to a formation of Bettys, and Kernan's fire set one bomber's wing tanks ablaze and sent it into the sea. When Phillips switched on his running lights so the fighters could form up, a Betty slipped in among them behind O'Hare. Phillips radioed a warning and Kernan fired at the bomber, which fired back. Skon reported: "I saw Mr O'Hare's lights go out and, at the same instant, he seemed to veer off and slant down into darkness." O'Hare's plane was never found. He was listed as missing and declared dead a year later. For decades the usual story was that friendly fire from the Avenger killed him, based on a 1962 history of Enterprise. A 1997 study by Steve Ewing and John Lundstrom concluded that the Betty's nose gunner most likely shot him down and that Kernan had been wrongly blamed. O'Hare was already famous. On 20 February 1942, defending USS Lexington, he attacked a formation of Betty bombers alone after his wingman's guns jammed. He was credited with five, though the records show three, and became the Navy's first ace of the war. His name outlived the war: on 19 September 1949 the city's Orchard Field was renamed O'Hare International Airport in his honor. The Navy also named a destroyer, USS O'Hare, for him.

This is U.S. Navy film of Fighting Squadron 3 over Oahu in spring 1942: O'Hare's own Wildcat, marked F-13, flying with his commander John Thach's F-1. There is no film of the night action of 26 November 1943. The original film is silent.
