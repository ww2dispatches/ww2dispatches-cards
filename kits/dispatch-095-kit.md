Sunk by Her Own Crew, Then Sunk Again: Strasbourg at Toulon #shorts

## YouTube Shorts description
Her own crew sank this battleship in 1942. The Italians raised her, and U.S. bombers sank her again in 1944.

At 4 a.m. on 27 November 1942, German combat groups drove into Toulon. Their mission, Operation Lila, was to seize the French fleet, as Germany took over the rest of Vichy France after the Allied landings in North Africa. The fleet's commander was Admiral Jean de Laborde, flying his flag in the new battleship Strasbourg. He was hostile to the British and to de Gaulle, and after the landings he had even proposed sailing out to attack the Allies. Since 11 November standing orders from Vichy's navy minister, Admiral Gabriel Auphan, said the ships must be scuttled rather than fall into foreign hands. The Germans caught the port admiral, André Marquis, in bed at Fort Lamalgue. German tanks rolled through the main gate of the naval base at 5:25 a.m., and the signal went out to the ships: "Scuttle! Scuttle! Scuttle!" Crews opened the sea valves and set off demolition charges. When a German officer reached Strasbourg and demanded that Laborde hand her over, the admiral answered that his ship was already sunk. About 77 vessels were destroyed, among them 3 battleships, 7 cruisers, 15 destroyers and 13 torpedo boats. Twelve Frenchmen were killed and 26 wounded. Hitler's prize was gone. The Germans took only 39 small ships and a few disarmed destroyers and damaged submarines, and the scuttled ships never fought for either side. Strasbourg's story did not end that day. The Italians refloated her in 1943, and U.S. bombers sank her again on 18 August 1944 during the liberation of southern France. That is how Navy cameramen found her a few weeks later.

This is U.S. Navy film of the wreck of Strasbourg at Toulon in September 1944, a few weeks after U.S. bombers sank her the second time. It is not film of 27 November 1942. The original film is silent.
