## YouTube Shorts description
On 10 November 1944, Gen. Dwight D. Eisenhower, the Supreme Allied Commander, went to the front lines of the U.S. Ninth Army on the German and Dutch border. At Herzogenrath, Germany, he climbed out of his jeep, pulled off his raincoat, and was greeted by Maj. Gen. Leland Hobbs of the 30th Infantry Division. Near Eigelshoven in the Netherlands, he walked a muddy wood to talk with men of the 29th Infantry Division. These were the men about to make Operation Queen, the big push toward the Roer River, which opened on 16 November with one of the heaviest air attacks of the war in support of ground troops.

This is real U.S. Army Signal Corps film of the visit, shot on 10 November 1944: his jeep arriving, Eisenhower greeting the generals, and Eisenhower among the troops in the wet woods. The original film is silent.

Film: U.S. Army Signal Corps, 111-ADC-2674, U.S. National Archives (catalog.archives.gov/id/16478), public domain.
Sources: Operation Queen, Wikipedia.
