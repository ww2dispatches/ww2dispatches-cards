## YouTube Shorts title
The Only Road Into Starving Leningrad Was a Frozen Lake #shorts

## YouTube Shorts description
In Leningrad, anyone who wasn't a worker got about three thin slices of bread a day. This frozen lake was the only way in.

Since September 1941 German and Finnish forces had cut Leningrad off by land. Frozen Lake Ladoga became the city's only link to the rest of the Soviet Union. From 20 November 1941 the bread ration fell to its lowest level of the siege: 250 grams a day for workers and 125 grams, about three thin slices, for everyone else. On 22 November 1941 a column of 60 trucks led by Major V. A. Porchunov set out across the ice. It was the first motor convoy on the ice road, known as Military Road No. 101 and later as the Road of Life. Most of the trucks were GAZ AA "polutorkas", light trucks rated at a ton and a half. By the most common account, the first run brought back 33 tons of flour. The stakes were the city itself. Deaths from hunger and cold climbed to about 100,000 a month in January and February 1942. On 25 December 1941, as supplies began to arrive over the ice, the ration was raised for the first time: 350 grams for workers and 200 grams for others. From November 1941 to April 1942 the ice road carried more than 350,000 tons of cargo into Leningrad, most of it food.

This is Soviet film of the Lake Ladoga ice road from the winter of 1941 to 1942, as used in the U.S. War Department film The Battle of Russia (1943): trucks crossing the snow, tractors breaking a track across the ice, and flour sacks on the way in. The shots are not dated to 22 November 1941. The U.S. film carries narration and music, so this clip runs silent.

Film: The Battle of Russia (U.S. War Department, 1943), 111-OF-5 reel 8, U.S. National Archives (catalog.archives.gov/id/36071), public domain.
