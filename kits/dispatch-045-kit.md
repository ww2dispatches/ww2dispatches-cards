## YouTube Shorts description
In the early morning darkness of 8 November 1942, American and British troops began landing in French North Africa in Operation Torch. About 107,000 Allied troops took part, landing around Casablanca in French Morocco and at Oran and Algiers in Algeria. The defenders were the forces of Vichy France, and nobody knew for certain whether they would fight. They did, hardest of all around Casablanca. It was the U.S. Army's first large scale amphibious landing under hostile fire. Most of the fighting was over within three days, and Casablanca surrendered on 11 November.

This is real U.S. Navy newsreel film of the landings: shell splashes off the coast, carrier planes taking off, and wrecked ships in Casablanca harbor. Individual shots are not dated. The original film is silent.

Film: U.S. Navy, WW-II Navy Newsreel #30, 428-NPC-44333, U.S. National Archives (catalog.archives.gov/id/87706), public domain.
