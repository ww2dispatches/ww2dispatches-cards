A 120 Knot Wind Over Tokyo: The First B-29 Raid #shorts

## YouTube Shorts description
The first B-29s over Tokyo flew into a wind of about 120 knots. It blew their bombs off course, and only 24 of 111 bombed the plant.

At 06:15 on 24 November 1944, Brig. Gen. Emmett O'Donnell Jr., commander of the 73rd Bombardment Wing, took off from Isley Field on Saipan in the B-29 "Dauntless Dotty", leading the first B-29 strike on Tokyo. His copilot was Maj. Robert K. Morgan, who had flown the B-17 "Memphis Belle" over Europe. Dauntless Dotty was Morgan's own B-29 of the 497th Bomb Group, named for his wife, Dorothy. 110 more B-29s followed him, 111 in all, carrying 277.5 tons of bombs. It was the first raid on Tokyo since the Doolittle Raid of April 1942. The target was the Nakajima aircraft engine plant at Musashino, on the edge of Tokyo. Over Japan the bombers flew at 27,000 to 33,000 feet into winds of about 120 knots, the jet stream. Bombs blew off course, and it proved nearly impossible to aim from that height. Seventeen B-29s turned back. Only 24 bombed the Musashino plant; 64 more dropped on docks and city areas. Strike photos showed just 16 bomb bursts in the target area. The results were judged disappointing. Early in 1945 Curtis LeMay replaced Gen. Haywood Hansell at XXI Bomber Command, and in March he sent the B-29s in low, at night, with incendiaries.

This is U.S. Army Air Forces film of B-29s of the 497th Bomb Group, Dauntless Dotty's group, on a later mission to the same Nakajima target at Tokyo, probably spring 1945, flying past Mount Fuji. It is not film of 24 November 1944. The original film is silent.

Film: Army Air Forces Combat Subjects, 18-CS-3655, Bomb Nakajima Plant, Tokyo, U.S. National Archives (catalog.archives.gov/id/5012), public domain.
