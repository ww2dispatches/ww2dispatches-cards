## YouTube Shorts description
Late on the afternoon of 21 November 1943, the second day on Betio, Col. David M. Shoup sent his situation report to the 2nd Marine Division. It ended: "Casualties many; percentage dead not known; combat efficiency: We are winning." Shoup, 38 and the division's junior colonel, had been given command of the assault just before the battle. On D Day his amtrac was hit and a shell fragment wounded his leg, and he stood waist deep in water beside the long pier working his radio to get units ashore. By the second afternoon Marines had crossed the airfield to the south shore. Organized resistance ended on 23 November. Shoup received the Medal of Honor and in 1959 became the 22nd Commandant of the Marine Corps.

This is U.S. Navy color film from D Day, 20 November 1943, the day before Shoup's message: Marines in amtrac 67, men heading for the beach, and smoke over Betio. The original film is silent.

Film: U.S. Navy, 428-NPC-12205, Landings on Tarawa, U.S. National Archives (catalog.archives.gov/id/79255), public domain.
Sources: Col. Joseph H. Alexander, Across the Reef: The Marine Assault of Tarawa, Marine Corps Historical Center (1993); Capt. James R. Stockman, The Battle for Tarawa, HQ US Marine Corps (1947, HyperWar).
