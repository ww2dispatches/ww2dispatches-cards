## YouTube Shorts description
The 104th Infantry Division, the Timberwolves, attacked northeast of Stolberg, Germany, on 16 and 17 November 1944 as the left wing of VII Corps. Its commander, Maj. Gen. Terry Allen, had drilled it in night fighting; Omar Bradley later called it the only division he knew that was prepared for night combat. Its goal was the Donnerberg, Hill 287, guarded by mines, wire, and three giant pillboxes. On 17 November Allen's guns fired 5,621 rounds in support, yet the 414th Infantry made little headway. Around noon the next day the defense cracked, and that night one company slipped almost 1,000 yards through the dark Eschweiler woods to seize a road junction.

This is real U.S. Army Signal Corps film from the VII Corps front on 17 November 1944: a rifleman firing from cover beside a burning building (a neighboring regiment, not the 104th), and Sherman tanks on the northeast edge of Stolberg. The original film is silent.

Film: U.S. Army Signal Corps, 111-ADC-2719 and 111-ADC-2709, U.S. National Archives (catalog.archives.gov/id/16523 and /16513), public domain.
Sources: Charles B. MacDonald, The Siegfried Line Campaign, Chapter XVIII, US Army Center of Military History; Timberwolves: The Story of the 104th Infantry Division, Stars and Stripes G.I. Stories (Lone Sentry); Terry de la Mesa Allen Sr., Wikipedia.
