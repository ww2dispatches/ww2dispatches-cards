## YouTube Shorts description
Operation Queen opened on 16 November 1944 with the largest air attack of the war in direct support of ground troops, and at 12:45 the US First and Ninth Armies went in toward the Roer River. The bombs left most forward German positions intact. For the 1st Infantry Division's 16th Infantry, the way to the village of Hamich ran through woods under heavy shelling: by the next day rifle companies of its 1st Battalion that had started with about 160 men were down to 60 or 70. On 18 November the regiment cleared Hamich house by house, and Hill 232 above it fell after fifteen artillery battalions fired on it at once.

This is real U.S. Army Signal Corps film shot in Hamich on 20 November 1944, four days after the attack began: 1st Division infantry moving past burning houses and through the village. The original film is silent.

Film: U.S. Army Signal Corps, 111-ADC-2715, U.S. National Archives (catalog.archives.gov/id/16519), public domain.
Sources: Charles B. MacDonald, The Siegfried Line Campaign, Chapters XVII and XVIII, US Army Center of Military History; Operation Queen, Wikipedia.
