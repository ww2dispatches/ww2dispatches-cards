## YouTube Shorts title
Patton Attacks in the Rain: Lorraine, 9 November 1944 #shorts

## YouTube Shorts description
On 8 November 1944, Lt. Gen. George S. Patton's Third Army opened a new offensive in Lorraine, aimed at Metz and the German border. The weather was so bad that Maj. Gen. Manton Eddy, commanding XII Corps, asked Patton to postpone. Patton told him to attack as scheduled or name his successor. The Germans thought the weather was too bad for an attack, so the assault caught them by surprise. Seven inches of rain fell in Lorraine that November, about twice the normal amount, and trench foot put almost as many men out of action that month as enemy fire. Third Army closed the ring around Metz on 19 November.

This is real U.S. Army Signal Corps film shot on 9 November 1944 near Delme, south of Metz: tanks and infantry moving up a tree lined road, a GI firing a machine gun, and men of the 35th Infantry Division searching German prisoners. The original film is silent.

Film: U.S. Army Signal Corps, 111-ADC-2678, U.S. National Archives (catalog.archives.gov/id/16482), public domain.
Sources: Christopher R. Gabel, The Lorraine Campaign: An Overview, September to December 1944, Combat Studies Institute, U.S. Army Command and General Staff College, 1985; Battle of Metz, Wikipedia; Lorraine campaign, Wikipedia.
