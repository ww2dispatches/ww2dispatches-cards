## YouTube Shorts description
On 27 October 1945, eight weeks after Japan surrendered, the warships that won the war lay at anchor in New York for Navy Day. President Harry Truman spoke at the commissioning of the carrier USS Franklin D. Roosevelt, reviewed the fleet from the destroyer Renshaw, and went aboard USS Missouri, where Japan had signed the surrender.

This is real U.S. Navy film from that day: warships firing a salute with blimps overhead, the President aboard Missouri, and sailors drawn up on her deck. The original film is silent.

Film: U.S. Navy, "Navy Day: President Truman boards USS Missouri", 428-NPC-15343, U.S. National Archives (catalog.archives.gov/id/79988), public domain.
Sources: Address on Foreign Policy at the Navy Day Celebration in New York City, American Presidency Project; Navy Day 1945, Truman Library Institute.
