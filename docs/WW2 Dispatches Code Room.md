# WW2 Dispatches: Code Room

**Code Room · a Declass daily puzzle.** A daily puzzle inside Declass that feeds the same Intel and card economy. Instruction lines: "Find the links between the files" and "Crack today's files". This doc holds the decided rules and how they're built. The Declass game itself is in the Declass Build doc and the Envelope doc; the weekly master list upkeep is in the Analytics doc ("Code Room master list"). This doc has two copies, the Project doc and `docs/WW2 Dispatches Code Room.md` in the cards repo, kept the same.

**Where the work happens (from 7 Nov 2026):** code, debugging, and preview testing in the **Code Room: Build** chat; rules, wording, and decisions in the **Code Room: Planning** chat. The earlier development history is in the chat "Code room daily puzzle dev". The old pinned Intercept chat is retired.

**The name (decided 2 Nov 2026).** Called Intercept from 26 Oct to 2 Nov 2026, then renamed Code Room as a trademark precaution: INTERCEPT GAMES is a live Take-Two Interactive registration in the game classes (9, 41, 42), and Codex, Enigma, Decipher, and Cypher were all taken or crowded in the code breaking realm. Code Room is a plain descriptor, the name of a room inside Declass, not a product, the same reasoning as Campaigns. The subtitle "a Declass daily puzzle" stays. Only the player facing name changed: the code, the data file (`functions/_data/intercept.json`), the API paths, the tables, the preview link, and the test tools keep the intercept name.

## The rules (decided 26 Oct 2026)
* Single player only. No player vs player, rankings, percentile, or leaderboards.
* One puzzle per game day, ending at midnight Eastern; a new one deals the next day. No clock.
* 12 cards in four hidden groups of three, laid out three across. Card faces only, no story text or tags. Tap and hold any card to see it full size.
* Select three and tap "File it". Correct: the three clip together with a typed label showing the link. Wrong: the cards shake and a mistake mark gets a red REJECTED stamp.
* 3 mistakes allowed. No near miss hint. The same three again doesn't count ("Already tried"). Once three groups are found the last locks itself. Running out of mistakes ends it and reveals every group.
* **Score:** 25 per group found, minus 10 per mistake. A solved puzzle scores 100, 90, 80, or 70. No speed or order bonus, no visible difficulty.
* **Rewards, paid on the result screen:** 3 Intel for playing, plus 15 for 100, 10 for 90, 5 for 70 or 80, 2 if not solved. Every solve picks any one of the 12 cards, owned or not (unowned cards carry a wax seal and glow). With mistakes: normal still odds. Perfect: Silver or better (Silver 79%, Gold 20%, Top Secret 1%). A finish already held converts to Intel. Not solved: no card. Field manual line: "Solve it, pick a card. Solve it perfectly, it's Silver or better."
* **Card pool:** every card type, only cards already posted. Sensitive cards can appear; a link must never treat tragedy as a gimmick.
* **Random grids:** every player gets their own grid from the master list: one easy, two medium, one hard group, three cards each, with a conflict check. Easy is visible in the photo, medium knowable from the place or subject, hard is shared operations, ships, commanders, and clever links. Printed details (date, series stamp) are never links; at most one deliberate decoy per grid. A group needs 3 posted cards to be used, ideally 5 or more. Favor cards the player doesn't own and groups not seen recently. Hard groups don't repeat for a player within 90 days (oldest first when unavoidable). Every link historically airtight.
* **Saved state:** a dealt grid is saved and can't be rerolled. One row per player per day; rows deleted after 90 days except the hard groups seen. Nothing personal stored.
* **Other:** doesn't count toward the streak. Placement near the top of the Declass home screen. Share picture: the completed groups with their cards and links, plus the result. A guided first puzzle for new players. Look: files on the dark Night Dispatch desk, in the Code Room's own room, the visual pass's "Night Desk" option A (the same dark desk and scanlines as the rest of Declass, lit by a cool blue lamp near the top instead of the Darkroom's warm red glow; decided 30 Oct 2026 so the puzzle feels distinct), paperclips, File it, clipped groups with typed labels, REJECTED stamps, wax seals.
* **Legal guardrails:** never use the name Connections anywhere public, including "like Connections" in marketing; no yellow, green, blue, and purple difficulty colors; no grid of word tiles; our own wording (no "one away"); our own result picture.
* **Not doing:** percentile or rankings, the same grid for everyone, a clock or speed bonus, a near miss hint, visible difficulty, a Top Secret order bonus, a next day debrief, player vs player. The Daily Operation idea grew into the parked Campaigns mode (Campaigns doc, 30 Oct 2026); a poker style mode was dropped.

## How it is built
The rules are in the synopsis from the 26 Oct strategy chat (twelve cards, four hidden groups of three, File it, three mistakes, 25 a link minus 10 a mistake, Intel and a card pick on the result screen). How they are built:

**Launch switch:** `INTERCEPT_LIVE` at the top of `functions/_lib/intercept.js`. While it is `false`, the home page shows "Code Room · Daily puzzle · Coming soon" as a slim line with no counter, the Field manual section is hidden, and the server refuses to deal. Launching is a one line change and a push, no Cloudflare step. **Preview:** a tester can play it now in one browser by opening ww2dispatches.com/declass?intercept=preview once (?intercept=off turns it off). It is remembered in that browser and sent as the `x-intercept-preview` header; the server honors it only for testers (`interceptOpen`).

**Who counts as a tester (merged 7 Nov 2026):** a player flagged tester in the database (anyone who signed in before the Declass launch), or a player whose file number is on a list. The lists live in `functions/_lib/admins.js`: the built in admin list (Brian, WKB9MK5Y) plus the `ADMIN_FILES` variable, which also open the admin console, and a new `TESTER_FILES` variable, which opens tester previews only, never the console. Both variables are comma separated file numbers in Cloudflare (Pages project, Settings, Variables and Secrets, Production). A file number comes from the scrambled Google id, so it survives the launch night wipe as long as `ID_SALT` never changes; adding a tester is a variable change, not a database edit. The admin console (`functions/api/stats.js`) now reads the same list, so there is one place to change it. The preview still needs the ?intercept=preview flag in that browser, so without it Brian sees exactly what players see.

**Preview branches:** a branch deployed to its own pages.dev address (for example `visual-pass.ww2dispatches.pages.dev`) needs that address added to the "Declass web" Google client's Authorized JavaScript origins before Google sign in works there. Production (ww2dispatches.com and www) is already on the client.

**No setup from Brian:** the server creates its two tables itself the first time the puzzle is used (`ensureTables`). `migrations/0003_intercept.sql` holds the same statements for the record.

**The master list:** `functions/_data/intercept.json`, next to the catalog and bundled with the server, never served. Each group: `id`, `link`, `difficulty`, `cards` (dealt from), `alsoFits` (fits the link but never dealt in it, so it stays off any board holding the group), `source`, `added`, optional `vetoed`. The Tuesday analytics run maintains it (Analytics doc, "Code Room master list"), moved there from the Wednesday research run on 27 Oct 2026 so it only ever works from the week that has closed. First list: 20 groups (3 easy, 9 medium, 8 hard), every link checked against the records and, for the ship namesakes, the Navy's DANFS histories.

**The posted only rule (decided 27 Oct 2026):** the list names only cards that have already posted (a Name This card once its answer is public). Never a planned or scheduled card, because a day can still be dropped or swapped at the Saturday review, and the game must never hold a link to a card that never existed. The check tool refuses any other id and `--prune` removes it. The Tuesday run adds the cards that posted in the week just closed, Sunday through Saturday, so a card reaches the list about a week after it posts; the run also catches posts added by hand, such as a Flash day's extra posts. Three layers keep a dropped day out of the game: the record never gets a rendered image unless the Friday run built it, the catalog only holds cards with an image, and the server only deals a card after its post time has passed. The master list is the fourth, so the puzzle never even plans around an unposted card. The earlier, fuller list is kept as `tests/declass/intercept-fixture.json` for the server tests only.

**The deal** (`dealGrid`): one easy, two medium, one hard group from those with 3 or more posted cards; three cards each, favoring cards the player doesn't own (3 to 1) and cards not dealt to them in the last 30 days; groups seen in the last week or month weighted down. Hard groups never repeat within 90 days while a fresh one can deal; when none can, the one seen longest ago comes first. Checks on every board:
* no card on the board fits two of the four links (`cards` plus `alsoFits`),
* a series stamp or year on exactly three cards that are a real group is refused (the board would give it away by reading),
* at most one decoy: a series, a year, or another master list link holding exactly three cards from different groups,
* Living Cards (film posted) are dealt as the living variant 1 in 6, shown by their still frame.
A deal checks at most 600 boards, which keeps it inside Cloudflare's CPU allowance (under 1 ms on average, about 6 ms worst case on the October catalog). If none passes, the page says the files are still being sorted and offers a retry.

**Server** (`functions/api/intercept/`): `GET /api/intercept` (today's puzzle without dealing), `POST start` (deals once and saves the grid, so closing the page never rerolls it; six starts at once all get one grid), `POST guess` {day, pick} (correct, wrong, already tried, or finished; a compare and set on a move counter so parallel guesses can't both land; no near miss hint; the fourth group locks itself; when it ends, Intel is paid in the same batch), `POST claim` {day, index} (solved only, once, guarded by an `i:<day>` key in the opened table; a Flash card can't be claimed; perfect solve uses the Silver or better table in `odds.js`; a finish already held converts to Intel like any duplicate; a Top Secret takes the next worldwide serial; the card waits to be turned). `state` carries a small `intercept` summary for the home tile. The puzzle day ends at midnight Eastern; a guess on yesterday's file is refused as expired, and an unfinished puzzle pays nothing. Rows are deleted after 90 days; each player's list of hard groups seen (`intercept_seen`) is kept for the no repeat rule. Delete my dossier removes both. Playing never touches the streak. Intel shows in the log as "Code Room puzzle".

**Page** (`site/declass/intercept.js` and `intercept.css`, hooked into `declass.js` through `window.Declass`): the home tile under Today's files (Coming soon, Crack today's files, In progress, Cracked with the score, Not cracked); the Code Room screen on the dark Night Dispatch desk: the intro with the closing time (and "This file closes in N minutes" in the last hour), the guided first puzzle (three short steps, once), twelve cards three across, tap to select (a paperclip and a lift), tap and hold for full size, Clear and File it, REJECTED stamps on the three mistake marks, solved groups clipped together over a typed label; the result with the CRACKED or FILES CLOSED stamp, score, Intel line, every link, and Pick a card (wax seal and a soft glow on cards not in the dossier, a Living Card tag, Flash cards greyed out); the pick opens a small CODE ROOM envelope and the normal turn and reveal, then returns to the puzzle. The share picture shows the four groups with their cards and links and the result, and the caption ("Cracked today's Code Room files: 90, 1 mistake. ww2dispatches.com/declass #Declass") is copied too. The Field manual has a Code Room section, shown once the puzzle is open to the player.

**Tests:** `tests/declass/intercept.mjs` (42 server checks against a local server and throwaway database), `tests/declass/intercept-check.mjs` (the master list check and readiness report, also used by the Tuesday run), `tests/declass/preview-access.mjs` (14 checks of who can open the preview after launch, in plain node with no server, since the server suite's test clock opens the puzzle to everyone), and a headless phone browser walkthrough of the whole flow. The six day hard group check retries a "no grid" deal up to three times, as the page's retry does (fixed 7 Nov 2026; it had failed one day in about 40% of runs). Five runs after the fix: 42 of 42 each.

**Readiness:** the check calls it ready at 98% fair deals, 40 different sets of groups, and 15 live hard groups. On 27 Oct: 19 of 20 groups live, 399 of 400 deals fair, but only 6 different sets of four groups and 25 different cards, because the October cards overlapped heavily (most naval). On 7 Nov: 399 of 400 fair, 78 sets of groups, 38 different cards, 14 live hard groups (ready once the 15th is live).

(2) Living Cards show their still frame in the grid, and a pick gives the living variant at the living odds (a perfect solve: Silver 65%, Gold 31.25%, Top Secret 3.75%).
