# German footage search, 19 Nov 2026

A deep search for German camera film inside U.S., British or Canadian government films and Universal Newsreel, under Brian's rule of 13 Nov 2026 (caption it as German film and name the film it came from; picture only). Searched the NARA catalog for training films, finished War Department and OWI films, British Crown films held at NARA, and the Universal Newsreel library. Downloaded and sheeted 31 files.

## Bottom line
* **Two usable new sources:** the British and U.S. Army aircraft recognition films on the Ju 88 and the Fw 190. Good, sharp film of the German aircraft, some of it German camera film.
* **The Universal Newsreel library holds many German subjects, but NARA has digitized them only as washed out VHS reference copies with a timecode burned into the picture.** Not postable. Better copies would need a NARA reproduction order.
* **The Nazi Plan** (1945, the U.S. prosecution's film of German footage for Nuremberg) is digitized only as the same poor VHS copies. The proper copies (238.1, 238.4, 111-ADC-8496 to 8547) are not digitized.
* Most of the prewar German film in the Universal library is plain German newsreel (Bavaria Tonwoche) with German titles, held as exchange footage, not cut into a Universal release. It was bought from Bavaria, not captured, so even the 19 Nov rule (captured film held by NARA) doesn't clearly cover it, and the copies are poor anyway. Left out; Brian's call.
* **Update, 19 Nov evening:** Brian opened NARA's own captured German reels and photos. The first captured reels are indexed in `captured-german-reels-2026-11-19.md`; German News No. 88 (the Gran Sasso raid and Hitler meeting Mussolini, Sep 1943) is the standout.

## 1. Recognizing the Ju 88 (111-TF-1-3684, naId 6044531, 7 min 18 s)
https://catalog.archives.gov/medialz/mopix/111/tf/111-tf-1-3684.mp4 (NARA file name may differ; use the catalog record)
* Made by the British Army Kinematograph Service with the Admiralty and Air Ministry ("Crown copyright, approved December 1943"), issued to U.S. forces as an Army training film. British government film; Crown copyright has expired. Narration (British) throughout; run silent.
* **00:54 to 01:30: German film. Ju 88s on a German airfield with crews at work, a Ju 88 taking off, a formation in flight seen from another bomber, a stick of bombs falling.** Caption: "German film, from the British training film Recognizing the Ju 88 (1943)".
* 01:36 to 03:54: a Ju 88 in flight filmed air to air and from the ground, probably a captured aircraft flown for the film; white graphic outlines are drawn over it at 03:12 to 03:48 (avoid those seconds). Clean passes 02:06 to 03:06.
* Serves Blueprint (the Ju 88), the Blitz and Battle of Britain dates (with care: not dated film).

## 2. Recognizing the Focke-Wulf 190 (111-TF-3681, naId 6044534, 6 min 56 s)
* Same makers (approved September 1943). The Fw 190 the RAF captured intact in June 1942, flown for the camera; Allied film of a German aircraft, not German film. Caption: "A captured Fw 190 filmed by the British, 1943".
* 01:00 to 01:36: the Fw 190 against cloud and over English fields; 01:42 to 02:06: alongside another aircraft; 02:36 to 03:00 and 03:12 to 03:30: clean passes. White graphic circles and outlines at 02:12 to 02:30, 03:06 and 03:36: avoid.
* Serves Blueprint (the Fw 190, the BMW 801 radial) and 23 Jun 1942 (the Fw 190 landed by mistake in Wales, verify).

## Where German film could still come from
* **NARA reproduction orders** of the Universal subjects above and of The Nazi Plan (paid; Brian's decision).
* **Canada's National Film Board, World in Action series** (1940 to 1945: Churchill's Island, Warclouds in the Pacific, The War for Men's Minds, Inside Fighting Russia). They are full of German film, and Canadian government film fits the rule. Not at NARA; the NFB streams them, and some are on Wikimedia Commons. Brian would download.
* **British Ministry of Information shorts on Commons** tagged PD-UKGov, for example Germany Calling (1941, the Lambeth Walk film) and These Are the Men (1943), both built from German film. Brian would download from Commons.
