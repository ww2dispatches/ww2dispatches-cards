# WW2 Dispatches: Newsletter

The rules for **The Weekly Dispatch**, the WW2 Dispatches newsletter on Substack. **Since 30 Oct 2026 each issue is one full length feature article**, about the most compelling story from that week's dates, written to read like a piece in a historical journal: real research, real archival photos with captions, and a sources section. It is not a recap of the posts. The Wednesday research run picks and researches the story; the Friday cards run writes the article, the launch announcement, and the occasional caption mention (rules for the mention are in the Cards doc). Shared setup is in the Project Brief.

## Why
An email list is the one audience WW2 Dispatches owns outright. It survives algorithm changes and account problems, it's the best place for sponsorships and affiliate links at small sizes, and it's what makes the archive pay off later. That's why signups started the day before launch instead of waiting for months two and three.

## Picking the story
* **The pool:** any WW2 event (including the 1930s lead up and the aftermath) whose anniversary falls in the issue's week, Sunday through Saturday. It does not have to be one we posted. If it was a dispatch, the article must go well beyond the card.
* **The test:** the most compelling story of the week for a general reader: a clear human arc, stakes, surprise, a strong ending, and enough well documented detail and photos to fill a feature. Prefer stories told through named people. Vary the series and theater over the weeks, and never repeat an earlier issue's subject (check `newsletter/story-*.json`).
* Somber subjects are allowed when they are the right story; tell them with care and no hype.

## The story file (written by the Wednesday research run)
`newsletter/story-NNN.json` for the issue the coming Friday drafts (NNN is one more than the highest issue in `newsletter/`), covering the current Sunday through Saturday:
* `issue`, `week` (the Sunday and Saturday dates), `event`, `date`, `place`, `series`, `why` (one line on why it won), and `alternates` (one or two other strong stories that week, in one line each).
* `facts`: **20 to 40 verified facts**, each one or two plain sentences with its sources. Lean on primary and official sources first (NHHC, NARA, the service histories, the National WWII Museum, IWM, peer reviewed or university press work, period newspapers), with Wikipedia only alongside one of those. Every number, time, name, and quote is checked. Where sources disagree, the fact says so and gives the range.
* `quotes`: every quote the article may use, verbatim, with who said it, when, and the source. Quote only what a source prints word for word.
* `photos`: three to five public domain archival photos downloaded to `newsletter/img/NNN-<name>.jpg` (Commons, NARA, NHHC, Library of Congress, IWM non commercial excluded), each with `file`, `page`, `caption` (one line, what and when), and `credit` (holder and catalog number, "public domain"). At least one photo the account has not posted before when one exists. A portrait of a key person is worth having. Never a generated image.
* If the story cannot be fully sourced or photographed, research picks the alternate instead and says why.

## Issue format (the article)
* **Title** (the Substack title and email subject): the story's own headline, short and specific, like a journal feature ("The Last Stand of Taffy 3"). No "This Week in WW2 Dispatches".
* **Preview line** (the subtitle): one or two sentences that set up the story and make people open it.
* The **lead photo** with its caption, then the article.
* **The article: about 1,500 words** (1,300 to 1,800), in the WW2 Dispatches voice: clear, vivid, for a general reader, built as a story (open on a moment, set the scene, build, turn, aftermath, and a closing line that lands). Five to eight short subheads (`##`). Two to four more photos placed where the story reaches them, each followed by its caption as one italic line (the renderer turns it into the photo's own Substack image caption, so the drop cap lands on the article's first paragraph; no kicker line above the article). Quotes sparingly, verbatim from `quotes`.
* After a `---`, **"Also this week"** (for issue 001, "Also from our first two weeks"): one line per 8 a.m. dispatch of the week in date order, `**No. NNN · <Title>**, <date> · [Read on X](<links.x>)`, with a one line lead in. Saturday's dispatch links to the X profile, since it posts after the build.
* After a `---`, **Sources**: the main sources behind the article as linked titles with the publisher. Encode parentheses in URLs (%28 and %29).
* After a `---`, a two sentence **sign off** ("Thank you for reading..."), with a one line tease of next week only when the coming week's slate already shows a strong story, then *WW2 Dispatches*. **No Ko-fi line in the issue body:** the email footer carries it.
* **Facts come only from the story file** (and the listed dispatches' `facts` for the links list). If the article needs a fact the file lacks, the Friday run verifies it against a source first and adds it to the file, never from memory. No dashes.
* Optional extras when they come cheap: a book recommendation at the end (affiliate link later), sponsorships once the list grows.

## Workflow
1. The Friday cards run reads `newsletter/story-NNN.json` (if research did not write it, the Friday run picks and researches the story itself by the same rules and says so in its summary), writes the article to `newsletter/issue-NNN.md` in the cards repo, LOOKS at every photo it places, and records `"newsletter": NNN` in each dispatch JSON listed in Also this week (and in the dispatch the article is about, if there is one).
2. It runs `python3 renderer/newsletter_page.py` (the send date defaults to the coming Sunday), fixes every PROBLEM line, and publishes `newsletter/review-page.html` to the **Weekly Dispatch** page (below). NOTE lines for Saturday's posts linking to the X profile are expected.
3. It files a Schedule Board task due Saturday 09:00, **"Paste and schedule the weekly newsletter"**, linking the Weekly Dispatch page, plus a push reminder at that time (Brief, "Shared run procedures").
4. It renders and schedules the Sunday share post (below) and writes `newsletter/share-NNN.json`, before building the page so the page shows it.
5. During the Saturday review Brian opens the page, leaves notes or approves, then follows its Post it steps to paste the issue into Substack, add the two tags and subscribe buttons, and schedule it for Sunday 7 a.m. Mountain (about 10 minutes), and taps Mark scheduled.
6. Check that each caption pasted into the photo's own caption line and the drop cap sits on the first story paragraph. If a caption lands as a separate paragraph, use the photo's ··· menu, Edit caption, paste it there and delete the paragraph. If photos fail to paste, upload them in the Substack editor instead (Open full image under each one).

If an issue was already approved, set its status back to `review`.
* The Saturday 8 a.m. post hasn't gone out when the Friday run builds the issue, so its link points to the X profile. That is fine to send; the page lists them under Links to check.

Free every Sunday.", and "Read it free: ww2dispatches.substack.com". LOOK at both before scheduling.
* **Link:** Substack builds the post address from the title, so the run uses `https://ww2dispatches.substack.com/p/<title in lower case, words joined by hyphens, punctuation dropped>` (issue 001 would be /p/the-last-stand-of-taffy-3). The Post it steps tell Brian to keep the title as given.
* **Captions:** two sentences that tease the story without giving away the ending, then "Read it free:" and the link on X and Threads. **Facebook carries no link** and ends "Read it free at the link on our Page." (Meta caps some Pages at two link posts a month, counting links in comments, unless they pay for Meta One; the Page lists "The Weekly Dispatch, free every Sunday" under its About links). Instagram ends "Read it free at the link in our bio." One hashtag (#WW2) on X and Instagram only. Writing rules apply (no dashes; facts only from the story file). Alt text describes the card.
* **Record:** `newsletter/share-NNN.json` with `issue`, `time` (as shown, "Sun 8 Nov 2026, 10:00 a.m."), `link`, `tags`, `cards` (`x`, `instagram`), `caption` (X), `threads`, `facebook`, `instagram`, `alt`, and `buffer` (the four post ids once scheduled).
* **Tags** (Substack, two per issue): the series (Battle Lines, Home Front, Blueprint, Statecraft) and the theater (Pacific, Europe, Atlantic, North Africa, Eastern Front; none extra for a Home Front story). The run puts them in `tags`; the Post it steps show them. No tags for single battles, ships, or people.
* **Scheduling:** the Friday run schedules all four in Buffer (custom time, Sunday 10:00 Mountain), using the card raw GitHub URLs, then rebuilds the review page, which shows the card and caption under Post it. If Brian decides not to send an issue, the posts are deleted.

## Growing the list
* **After the first month:** check open rates and signups, and test the Sunday send time.
* **Substack recommendations (around month two):** once a few issues are out, Brian recommends a couple of other WW2 or history Substacks and asks them to recommend WW2 Dispatches back. This is the main way Substack's network sends new readers.
* **Daily posts on Substack:** not as a daily email, which would hurt the weekly issue. The only idea worth testing is Substack Notes (the short feed that surfaces a publication to people outside its list), with only the best card or video of the day, judged after two weeks by new subscribers. It depends on the Facebook versus Substack decision in the Roadmap's open decisions. Buffer lists a Substack channel, but what it posts is unconfirmed (likely Notes); Typefully also lists Substack.
* **Paid tier:** available any time with no gate (Substack keeps 10% plus Stripe fees), but only worth it with a large, engaged list. See the Roadmap's Subscriptions plan.
