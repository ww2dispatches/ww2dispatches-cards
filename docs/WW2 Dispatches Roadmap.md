# WW2 Dispatches: Roadmap

Started 15 Oct 2026. Goal: post every day from launch (Sat 17 Oct 2026) and keep Brian's weekly time to one short review session. This doc holds the posting layout, the big dates ahead, the open decisions, and the long term plans. What is scheduled and Brian's to dos are on the Schedule Board; the weekly runs are in the Project Brief's "Weekly workflow"; how posts are performing is on the Scoreboard (Analytics doc).

## Posting schedule at a glance
**The one place for the slot layout.** All times Mountain. This is the layout of the slots and which kind of post goes in each, not a list of specific posts. To see what is actually scheduled, and anything that needs Brian's hand, open the pinned **Dispatch Schedule** board, which reads Buffer and Typefully live.

**Every day**
* **8:00 a.m., story dispatch.** The full card with a long story caption. X, Instagram, Threads, Facebook. (Cards doc)
* **Noon, the daily video.** A Living Card (real archival film, whenever usable film exists) or a Story Card (archival photos, Onyx narration, no music). Short caption. X, Instagram (Reel), Threads, Facebook, TikTok, YouTube Shorts. (Videos doc)
* **5:00 p.m., evening card.** Photo of the Day, Quote of the Day, or Spec Sheet, with a shorter caption. X, Instagram, Threads, Facebook. No fixed weekday per format. (Cards doc)
* The four series are not tied to any time. Any series can run in any slot.

**Wednesday and Saturday only**
* **10:00 a.m., Name This.** X (into an X Community, through Typefully), Threads (History Threads community), Facebook Page, and Instagram (a two week test, see Open decisions), the last three through Buffer. No dispatch number. (Name This doc)

**Sunday**
* **8:00 a.m., the weekly newsletter** on Substack (first issue Sun 1 Nov 2026). (Newsletter doc)

That makes three posts a day on the card platforms (four on Wednesday and Saturday with Name This) and one a day on TikTok and YouTube. Everything publishes through Buffer except Name This on X (Typefully) and the newsletter (pasted into Substack by Brian). Each post also appears on the ww2dispatches.com home page at its time (Project Brief, "Domain and website").

They are the first place the Wednesday research run hunts for film for a Living Card; with no film, the noon video is a Story Card. The 5 p.m. card on an anniversary day is a good place for a Spec Sheet or Quote tied to the same event. Hashtag rules for anniversary days are in the Cards doc.

## Flash cards plan
A long term idea, agreed in principle 25 Oct 2026, not yet built into the weekly runs.
* **The name:** FLASH, the top designation on the WW2 news wires (AP's D Day flash in 1944 broke into network radio within seconds). It sits above the PRIORITY envelopes in Declass.
* **What it is:** the one card type that spans all four series at once (Battle Lines, Home Front, Blueprint, Statecraft). Rare, and a big moment when it lands.
* **When (decided):** Flash days are the Declass commemorative days, so a Flash card and a commemorative file always arrive together. The set list, ten Flash cards (1 and 2 September are two separate Flash cards sharing one commemorative file):
  1. 19 Feb 1945, the landing on Iwo Jima
  2. 8 May 1945, VE Day
  3. 4 Jun 1942, the Battle of Midway
  4. 6 Jun 1944, D Day
  5. 15 Aug 1945, VJ Day (Japan announces its surrender)
  6. 1 Sep 1939, Germany invades Poland
  7. 2 Sep 1945, the formal surrender aboard USS Missouri
  8. 15 Sep 1940, Battle of Britain Day
  9. 7 Dec 1941, Pearl Harbor
  10. 16 Dec 1944, the Battle of the Bulge begins
* **Debut (decided):** Pearl Harbor, Mon 7 Dec 2026, the 85th anniversary. **Everything the three runs need is built and pushed (25 Oct 2026):** the renderer's full art layout (`drawFlash` in `renderer/card.html`, both sizes, with a FLASH stamp on the video frames), the `flash` series value in the logs, the home page, the analytics, and the Declass catalog, the game's server and page changes, the prepared art and record in `flash/`, and the rules in the Research doc ("Flash days"), the Cards doc ("The Flash card"), and the Videos doc. The Wednesday 2 Dec research run reads those docs as its rulebook, so no change to the scheduled tasks themselves was needed.
* **In the Declass dossier (decided, built):** Name This is a half width tile like the four series, and a **Flash** tile sits beside it, edged in the Top Secret holographic tint. It shows "None posted yet" until the first Flash posts, then opens the Flash section. The catalog marks Flash cards with a `flash` field; All cards has a Flash filter.
* **Always Top Secret (decided):** a Flash card only ever comes in the Top Secret finish, with the holographic frame and sheen, which makes it unlike any other card in the game. It has a single finish slot. **No worldwide serial** (decided): every player gets one, and it should never be a race to log on first.
* **Everyone who plays gets it (decided, built):** the commemorative file on a Flash day holds **four cards: that day's Flash card plus three deep archive cards** (five on 1 and 2 September, both Flash cards). The 8 a.m. envelope that day also carries the Flash card as its guaranteed card, since it is that slot's dispatch (a second copy shows as ×2). Players who miss the file (it stays open three days) can still pull the Flash card later from archive pulls, at about **1 in 200 per archive card**. Flash cards never come in the starter pack or as an ordinary archive pick. This replaces the Envelope doc's "no guaranteed anniversary card" rule for commemorative files; the Envelope and Declass Build docs get updated when the server side is built.
* **Full art (direction, 25 Oct 2026):** Flash cards get an entire card design rather than the Field Telegram bands, in the spirit of full art trading cards: the image runs edge to edge and the type sits over it. A new graphic and image every year. First mockups 25 Oct 2026 (Flash Card Mockups artifact, Pearl Harbor on the USS Shaw explosion, NH 86118): Brian chose **C2, Kodachrome**, a colorized treatment in the warm, rich look of 1940s color slide film, for the posted card. The Declass version (the Top Secret frame with the palm fronds breaking over it) was rejected: a full art card must never sit inside the Top Secret frame.
* **In Declass (decided 25 Oct 2026):** take **A, borderless**. No frame and no rim: the art runs to every edge with rounded corners and a drop shadow, and a soft holographic shine drifts across the art and follows a finger or the phone's tilt. A thin holo rim (take B) and an etched line foil (take C) were passed over. If a future Flash photo has a pale edge that blurs into the cream dossier, research picks a darker crop rather than adding a rim. No swirl or wavy foil patterns, to stay clear of Pokémon's look.
* **Card layout from the mockup:** WW2 DISPATCHES wordmark and dispatch number top left on a dark fade, a cream FLASH tape stamp top right, the big date, place, and pull line at the bottom on a dark fade, handle bottom right. The same art and type serve the posted card.
* **Proposed, not yet decided:** on the posted card, a FLASH stamp in place of the series stamp; the 8 a.m. card on a Flash day becomes the Flash, with a four slide Instagram carousel (one slide per series angle); in Declass a Flash card counts toward all four series collections and gets its own reveal sound (a teletype burst).
* **Every year (decided):** each Flash date gets a new, revamped Flash card every year, with a new angle, new graphics, and a new image, so ten new Flash cards arrive each year. This is the one exception to the research rule that never repeats an event: the event repeats, the card never does.
* **Open:** the Flash reveal in the envelope (today it gets the Top Secret glow and fanfare; a teletype burst is the idea for later); a line for the attorney consult on the Flash design against Pokémon's trade dress; the 1 and 2 September file opens on 1 September, so a player who opens it that day gets only the Poland card (moving that file's opening to 2 September would give both; decide before September 2027); preparing each year's Flash art a few weeks ahead (the prepared art and record go in `flash/`; the Research doc says what happens if one is missing).

## Subscriptions plan
A long term goal, not a short term one. The model is @VoicesofWW2's X subscription ($2 a month, exclusive posts, "tell me what content you'd like to see more of"). On 16 Oct 2026 it showed 15 subscribers, about $30 a month before fees, so subscriptions only pay once the account is large. Growth comes first.

Requirements, verified 16 Oct 2026 (recheck before applying; platforms change these often):
* **X Creator Subscriptions** (help.x.com subscriptions policy): age 18 or older; complete profile; verified email; two factor authentication; X Premium; account active 3 months or more (mid January 2027 at the earliest); posted in the past 30 days; 2,000 or more active followers who have Premium; 5,000,000 or more organic impressions in the last 3 months; completed identity verification. Apply in Creator Studio, Subscriptions. Price is picked from X's set price points. Done so far: two factor authentication, identity verification, verified email, and X Premium with the blue check (verification confirmed 23 Oct 2026). Still to reach: three months active, 2,000 Premium followers, and 5,000,000 impressions.
* **YouTube channel memberships:** 500 subscribers, 3 uploads in the last 90 days, and either 3,000 watch hours in 12 months or 3 million Shorts views in 90 days. YouTube keeps 30%. Possibly the first one reachable if Shorts take off.
* **Instagram Subscriptions:** 10,000 followers on a professional account (Creator or Business; the current Business account counts) in an eligible country. May require a Creator rather than Business account; verify then.
* **Facebook subscriptions:** check Meta's current requirements when the Page has a following.
* **TikTok:** subscriptions are tied to LIVE streaming and mostly invitation only. Skip; it doesn't fit the format.
* **Threads:** no creator subscription feature as of 16 Oct 2026.
* **Substack paid tier:** available any time with no gate; Substack keeps 10% plus Stripe fees. Only worth it with a large, engaged list.

Perks, chosen so a subscription never becomes a second job. Each one reuses work the weekly runs already do:
* **Early access:** subscribers see next week's dispatches the weekend before they post.
* **The weekly roundup** (the newsletter issue) as a subscriber post.
* **Topic vote:** once a month, subscribers pick a date or event for a future dispatch.
* **Blueprint notes:** one extra engineering detail per week that didn't fit the caption.

Starting price idea: $2 to $3 a month, in line with comparable accounts. Ko-fi memberships stay off until then; Ko-fi stays tips only.

## Archive plan
Why: old social posts are close to invisible because feeds favor new content, so an archive doesn't take views away from the social pages. It reaches people the feeds never will (someone searching "what happened on October 20 in WW2"), turns them into followers and subscribers, and is an asset the account owns.
* **When:** once there are a few hundred cards, roughly four to six months of two a day posting, so most calendar dates have something on them and the site looks full.
* **Build:** generated from the dispatch JSON, cards, and photos already in the ww2dispatches-cards repo, added to the existing home page in the repo's `site/` folder on Cloudflare Pages (live since 21 Oct 2026). The home page stays the front door, with a link into the archive.
* **Pages:** one page per calendar date (all years together), one page per dispatch, a page per series, and a page per evening format (all the Spec Sheets together make a strong Blueprint reference). A simple calendar view linked from the home page, which already shows today's posts. The performance log can feed a "most popular dispatches" page.
* **Every dispatch page sends people back to social:** the card, the story caption, the sources, and links to the original posts (plus TikTok and YouTube for videos), using the `links` field recorded since launch.
* **Every page carries the follow buttons, the newsletter signup, and the Ko-fi button.** The archive's job is to grow the accounts and the list, not to replace them.
* **Low effort upkeep:** the weekly runs rebuild the site from GitHub each week, so new dispatches appear on their own. Brian's weekly time doesn't change.
* Later options: search, a printable "this day" card shop on Ko-fi, and a classroom friendly version of the archive.
