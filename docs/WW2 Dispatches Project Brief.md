## The idea
A side income project, mostly for fun, that could grow into a real business. A "this day in history" World War II account across X, Instagram, Threads, Facebook, YouTube, and TikTok, plus a weekly email newsletter. It needs to stay low weekly effort so it fits around a full teaching schedule.

Inspiration: how @VoicesofWW2 on X operates, but with its own visual identity rather than copying their style or the Cards of History trading card look.

* **This day in history**, not a running real time timeline. Each post is tied to something that happened on that calendar date, any year of the era, and stands alone. It jumps around freely, which keeps it lively, and new followers can jump in anytime.
* **Scope:** broad WWII, not a narrow niche, and not limited to 1939 to 1945. Covers the road to war (1930s lead up), the war itself, and the aftermath into the late 1940s. Avoid putting "1939 · 1945" date ranges on brand assets.
* **Platform focus:** X, Instagram, Threads, and Facebook get two cards a day. A noon video goes out every day on all six channels, which keeps TikTok and YouTube Shorts active and lets Instagram Reels and Facebook Reels reach new people. **Video is the priority for growth.** The weekly Substack newsletter collects each week's dispatches for email readers.
* **Budget:** about $1,560 a year for Buffer plus Claude is acceptable. Spend is not a constraint on the daily video; if Claude usage becomes a problem, Claude says so and Brian upgrades.

## The four series
Each post carries one series label so followers know what flavor they're getting. Example: "WW2 Dispatches: Blueprint".
* **Battle Lines** (faded red #B04A3E): battles and military operations
* **Home Front** (olive green #5E6B3A): civilian life, rationing, factories, everyday people
* **Blueprint** (navy #3D5884): engineering and invention (radar, codebreaking machines, the jeep, industrial production). The signature angle, from an engineering teacher's perspective.
* **Statecraft** (brass gold #B8862B): politics and diplomacy, especially the 1930s lead up (Versailles, rise of the Nazi party, appeasement, Munich, Molotov Ribbentrop pact) and wartime conferences like Tehran and Yalta

The colors are locked. Each post uses its series color on the cream background. The series are not tied to any time slot. Name This uses its own color (Mimeograph Violet), not a series color.

## Writing rules (every post, every doc)
* Clear, engaging voice for a general audience. Short paragraphs, plain words.
* **No dashes of any kind.** Use a comma or end the sentence. Proper names like V-2 or B-17 are fine.
* **Verify before drafting.** Every date and fact is checked against at least one solid source, two for anything surprising. Never guess. Every source is listed in the dispatch JSON.
* Somber subjects (massacres, the Holocaust, heavy casualties, atrocities) never carry an engagement question, a Ko-fi soft ask, or a newsletter mention.

## Photos (every post)
* Lean on **public domain archival photography**. It is free and authentic. Use generated imagery only where no real photo exists, and never for Name This, Story Cards, or Spec Sheets.
* Prefer a photo of the event itself or from that time. If only a later photo exists, note it in the dispatch file and flag it to Brian.
* Good sources: NARA, U.S. military, Library of Congress FSA/OWI, IWM (Crown copyright expired), Wikimedia Commons files marked public domain. NARA still pictures (such as the 208 and 111-SC series) download reliably.
* **Photo fetching from Claude's cloud workspace:** Wikimedia rate limits Claude's workspace hard (429 errors, sometimes a full block for a while). What works: the English Wikipedia API (en.wikipedia.org/w/api.php, prop=images on an article, prop=imageinfo for license), Wikipedia's page summary service (en.wikipedia.org/api/rest_v1/page/summary/<Article>) for an article's lead photo, and downloading straight from upload.wikimedia.org by building the path from the md5 of the file name, using standard thumbnail sizes (960, 1280, or 1920 px). Space requests 20 seconds or more apart and expect some to fail. The Commons search API works now and then with long gaps. LOC, NHHC (history.navy.mil), and Metricool's file host are blocked from the shell. The NARA catalog search is intermittent; its own search works at https://catalog.archives.gov/proxy/records/search?q=... with an "Accept: application/json" header (use rows=, not limit=). The Internet Archive is blocked.
* **When a photo can't be fetched, ask Brian to download it and attach it in the chat**, giving him the exact page and image. In unattended runs, file a Schedule Board task with the page instead. Brian can supply photos quickly.
* Video specific sourcing (film, Wikimedia video) is in the Videos doc.

## Weekly workflow
Each batch covers the following Sunday through Saturday: **14 cards** (an 8 a.m. dispatch and a 5 p.m. evening card each day) and **7 noon videos**. Five scheduled runs in Claude's cloud, each feeding the next (no computer needed; Buffer, GitHub, and Typefully connected; automatic approval; push and email notification when done). **All five run on Opus 5.5, never Sonnet; any new WW2 Dispatches scheduled task gets Opus 5.5 too, and is made as a Claude Code task with both repos attached, never a chat task.** The order puts the video first, because video is the priority for growth: research picks each day's event for its film, the videos are built from that, and the cards follow.
1. **Tuesday 7:47 p.m. Mountain, "WW2 Dispatches weekly analytics review"** (follows the Analytics doc). Pulls every post's numbers from Buffer, scores them, updates the playbook the next three runs read, rebuilds the Scoreboard, files any decision for Brian, and sends him a short summary. 2. **Wednesday 7:47 p.m. Mountain, "WW2 Dispatches weekly research"** (follows the Research doc). Reads the playbook, picks all 14 slots with the 8 a.m. event chosen for the best usable film, verifies every fact into each dispatch's `facts` block, assigns and locks the dispatch numbers, downloads the film into the videos repo and the photos into `photos/`, tags the themes, writes the dispatch JSON and the slate with one or two alternates per 8 a.m. slot, files tasks for any film or photo Brian needs to supply (due Thursday 5 p.m.), and sends Brian the slate. **Brian may swap any day by telling Claude in this project before Thursday 7 p.m.**; the number stays. 3. **Thursday 7:47 p.m. Mountain, "WW2 Dispatches weekly video prep"** (follows the Videos doc). ## Record now, use later
A standing principle: whenever an idea for the future (archive site, newsletter, shop, analytics) would need data about past dispatches, start recording that data now, cheaply and automatically, so adding the feature later means building it, not digging back through months of posts. When a new long term idea comes up, ask what it will need from the dispatch record and add it here.

Fields kept in every dispatch JSON in `dispatches/`:
* **Content:** number, date, `time` ("08:00" or "17:00"), `format` (photo, quote, spec; absent on 8 a.m. dispatches), place, series, title, pull line, `body` (long caption, Instagram ending), `threads` (short caption), sources, photo file and its source page and license, framing (focusX, focusY, zoom), plus `quote`/`quoteBy` or `specTitle`/`specs` on evening cards. Which run writes which field is in the Research doc.
* **`facts`:** the verified fact sheet, six to twelve plain sentences each with its sources, written by the Wednesday research run. Every caption, script, hook line, and newsletter summary draws only from it (Research doc).
* **`film`:** the film record for the noon video (status, record, url, file, sound, shot notes, license, date note), written by research. **`kind`:** the planned noon video kind (`living` or `story`). **`somber`:** true on dispatches that never carry a question, soft ask, or newsletter mention.
* **`themes`:** what the dispatch is about (who, story, photo, theater, branch, nations), tagged by research, plus `video` {kind, opening, sound} added by the Thursday run once it has a noon video. Values only from `analytics/themes.md` (Analytics doc). Name This records carry the same block.
* **`videoPhotos`:** the extra public domain photos of the same event for that day's Story Card (file, page, credit), downloaded and lined up by the Wednesday research run; photo leads as links only on Living Card days.
* **`buffer`:** the Buffer post ids for `x`, `instagram`, `threads`, and `facebook`, saved by the Friday cards run when scheduled. Living Cards add `x_film`, `instagram_reel`, `threads_film`, `facebook_film`, `youtube`, `tiktok` and a `kit` block; Story Cards use a `story` block (see Videos), saved by the Thursday video run. The Tuesday review matches each post's numbers to its dispatch by these ids, so a reposted or replaced post must have its new id recorded.
* **`links`:** the live public URLs once a dispatch has posted: `x`, `instagram`, `threads`, `facebook`, plus `x_film`, `instagram_reel`, `threads_film`, `facebook_film`, `tiktok`, `youtube` for videos, and `posted` (YYYY-MM-DD). The Friday cards run fills these every week for anything that has posted, using only real URLs the services return; missing ones are left out and noted in the summary. Used by the home page, the archive site, and the newsletter.
* **`slides`:** the extra carousel photos with caption and credit, when the Instagram post is a carousel.
* **`question`:** true when the caption closes with an engagement question.
* **`soft_ask`:** true on the dispatch that carried the Ko-fi soft ask.
* **`newsletter_mention`:** true on a dispatch whose caption carried the occasional newsletter mention.
* **`newsletter`:** the issue number of the newsletter issue that covered the dispatch.
* Name This cards keep their own record in `namethis/` (see the Name This doc).

**The CSV logs** are the permanent record in spreadsheet form, rebuilt from the JSON by `python3 renderer/series_log.py` after every build run (Thursday, Friday, and Saturday) and committed with it. **Never edit them by hand**; to change what they record, change the JSON or the script. They open cleanly in Excel.
* `log/series-log.csv`: one row per card with dispatch number, post date and time, slot, series, format, event date, title, place, pull line, quote speaker or spec title, noon video kind (Living Card or Story Card), film source and whether it kept its sound, photo credit and page, carousel slides, the question, soft ask, and newsletter mention flags, newsletter issue, source count and source links, the theme tags, every live post link (X, Instagram, Threads, Facebook, the four video posts, TikTok, YouTube), the posted date, and the JSON file.
* `log/series-counts.csv`: running totals per series, morning and evening.
* `log/namethis-log.csv`: one row per Name This card with post and event dates, answer, place, clue, X Community, photo credit and page, sources, theme tags, and live links.
* `log/performance-log.csv`: one row per post per platform with its numbers, scores, and tags, rebuilt each Tuesday by `python3 renderer/analytics.py` (Analytics doc).
* When a new field is added to the JSON for a future idea, add it to the script too, so the CSV keeps up.
