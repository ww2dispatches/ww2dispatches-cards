# WW2 Dispatches: Videos

The rules for the **two daily videos**, at noon and 7 p.m. The two Thursday video runs follow this doc: the noon video run builds the week's noon videos, and the evening video run builds the week's 7 p.m. videos. The events, numbers, facts, film, and Story Card photos are chosen and secured by the Wednesday research run (Research doc); this doc covers turning them into videos. The films already on hand, with exact times for every usable shot, are in the Film Library doc. Shared setup (series, brand, writing rules, photo rules and workarounds, Buffer channel IDs, Schedule Board tasks, record keeping) is in the Project Brief. The slot layout is in the Roadmap. The card frame the videos share is in the Cards doc. What the analytics have taught so far is in `analytics/playbook.md` (Analytics doc); **read it before building the week's videos** and follow its patterns and any active test that touches video.

**This doc is the one place for how many videos each batch makes.**

## The goal
**Two videos every day, at noon and 7 p.m. Mountain**, on all six channels: X, Threads, Instagram Reels (shared to the feed), Facebook, YouTube Shorts, and TikTok. Video is the priority because it is the best way to reach new people on X, Instagram, TikTok, and YouTube (TikTok is growing fastest), which is why research picks each day's 8 a.m. event for its film first and plans the 7 p.m. video around film too (Research doc). The 7 p.m. video starts with the 15 to 21 Nov 2026 batch. Brian's weekly time should stay at about 45 minutes or less.

## Two kinds of video
* **Living Card:** real archival film playing in the card frame, labelled "Dispatch No. NNN · The Footage" on screen (`renderer/living.js` adds it). Keeps the film's own sound. **No AI label.**
* **Story Card:** archival photos with the Onyx narration, labelled "Dispatch No. NNN · The Story" on screen. **Always** says "Narration is AI generated" in the caption and is flagged AI generated in Buffer. Story Cards are a rare last resort for both slots.
* **Every video belongs to one card** and keeps that card's dispatch number. **The noon video always covers the 8 a.m. dispatch**, which research chose for its film; it never switches to another card's story. **The 7 p.m. video covers the card research planned it for** (Evening video, below).
* **No music, ever.** This is a period piece.

## Video count
* **Every day gets a noon video**, from the 25 to 31 Oct 2026 batch on, and **a 7 p.m. video**, from the 15 to 21 Nov 2026 batch on. Fourteen a week.
* **Brian always prefers a Living Card.** Whenever real usable film exists, the video is a Living Card, even if it waits on Brian's hand.
* **Film first:** research picks each 8 a.m. event for the best usable public domain film, plans each 7 p.m. video's source, and uploads the film to `footage/` on R2 (Research doc). Thursday makes a **Living Card** from the secured film, with no weekly limit. Videos research marked Story Card (no usable film exists) stay Story Cards. The event, number, and facts never change on Thursday.
* **Film that needs Brian (`needs brian`):** if the film is not in `footage/` on R2 when the Thursday run starts, after its media sweep (the cloud run can't reach the Source Film folder on Brian's computer), **do not build a Story Card for that video.** A Story Card built only to be replaced wastes usage. Leave the slot (noon or 7 p.m.) open in Buffer and file a Schedule Board task with a push reminder: "Open the WW2 Dispatches Project with your computer linked and ask Claude to cut the Living Card for No. NNN from Source Film", due **Saturday 9:00 a.m. Mountain** (Brian's review) or the evening before the post day if that comes first. Name the film file, the day, and the slot in the task and in the summary. Claude in the Project then stages the film from Source Film, cuts, renders, and schedules the Living Card per this doc (Nos. 033 and 037 are the model). **The Project chat can't reach R2**, so it commits the cut clip to the videos repo at `inbox/<filename>` (the next run's sweep moves it to `footage/<filename>` on R2; "Where videos live"), and the videos it renders take media.py's fallback into the videos repo; tell Brian that in the chat (the WARNING is expected there) and the next run's sweep moves them to R2.
* **Film that turns out unusable when cut:** if another usable film is known (the slate's alternates, the Film Library, or the Film sources below) and can be fetched, use it. If the only other film needs Brian, treat the video as `needs brian` above. For a 7 p.m. video, next try the evening order (a second cut of the noon film, Evening video below). Only when no usable film exists does the video become a **Story Card for the same card**.
* Film shot a few days before or after the event is fine **if the caption says so honestly** (Aachen, No. 009, is the model); research records this in the `film` block's `date_note`.
* The Thursday runs also fill any other empty slot of their own time (the noon run noon slots, the evening run 7 p.m. slots) in the next seven days, including the current week if a slot is still in the future. Never duplicate an existing video. A slot left open for a `needs brian` Living Card is not an empty slot to fill.
* **Video bank:** extra finished videos go into a bank (aim for 3 to 5 ready), so a hard week never leaves a gap. If a video's photos can't be fetched, use a bank video rather than pad. If there is none, file a Schedule Board task naming the photos Brian could supply and say so in the summary. The bank is listed in `status.md` in the cards repo. If a `needs brian` slot is still open on the evening before its day, a bank video may fill it.
* If a run is running long, finish and schedule videos in date order and say exactly what was left over and why.
* If the slate and dispatch files for the week are missing, follow "If a run fails" in the Research doc.

## Evening video (7 p.m., added 7 Nov 2026)
Research plans each day's 7 p.m. video in this order (Research doc, "Video picks first"); the Thursday evening run builds whatever the dispatch JSON says:
1. **Film of the 5 p.m. card's event:** a Living Card on that card. Its dispatch JSON has `"videoTime": "19:00"`, and its `film`, `video`, `kit` (or `story`), `themes.video`, and film keys under `buffer` work exactly as on an 8 a.m. dispatch.
2. **Film of the 2:30 p.m. Axis card's event:** the same, on the 2:30 card. Axis film follows the Film sources rules (captured Japanese film is fine; German and Italian newsreels never).
3. **A 5 p.m. card chosen for its film** (research made the filmed event the 5 p.m. card): built as in 1.
4. **A second cut of the noon film**, when research set `"second_cut": true` on the 8 a.m. dispatch (rules below).
5. **A Story Card** for the 5 p.m. or 2:30 card, only when research found no film for any of these; the summary says why.

**Second cuts.** A second Living Card from the same source film as the noon video, for the same 8 a.m. dispatch.
* **Different footage, never the same clip twice:** no shot from the noon clip appears in the second cut, and it opens on a different strong moving shot. Research's `film.shot_notes` says which stretch serves each video; cut from those. If the source has no second stretch worth posting, fall back to a Story Card per the order above and say so.
* **Its own words:** its own hook line and captions on every platform, sharing no sentence or distinctive phrase with the noon video or any card that day (Captions section).
* **Stored apart from the noon video**, so nothing is overwritten: the `video_b` block (same fields as `video`, with its own `footage` clip such as `footage/<id>-b.mp4`), the `kit_b` block and `kits/dispatch-NNN-b-kit.md`, `themes.video_b` (same fields as `themes.video`), and the Buffer ids under `buffer` as `x_film_b`, `instagram_reel_b`, `threads_film_b`, `facebook_film_b`, `youtube_b`, `tiktok_b`. Render with `node renderer/living.js <id> --cut=b`, which reads `video_b` and writes `videos/dispatch-NNN-b-x.mp4`, `-b-instagram.mp4`, `-b-threads.mp4`, `-b-vertical.mp4`, and the poster `videos/dispatch-NNN-b-instagram-poster.jpg`. A second cut always posts at 19:00. It shares the 8 a.m. dispatch's number and label ("Dispatch No. NNN · The Footage").
* **Declass uses only the noon video** as a card's Living variant; a second cut is a social post only.

## Major dispatches: the iconic shot rule
For huge moments in the war (MacArthur's return to Leyte is the model), always go after the iconic image and the iconic footage first, the photo and film people remember. If the famous shot can't be had (not digitized, copyright, no public domain copy), say so and offer the next best real footage. Big dates are the first place to hunt for film; research starts the hunt early for the dates in the Roadmap's "Upcoming big dates" so it isn't rushed.

## Vertical safe zone (1080 x 1920)
Tall phones zoom a 9:16 video to fill the screen and trim about 100 px off each side. TikTok's search bar and tabs cover the top 250 px, the app buttons cover a column down the right side from about the middle, and the username and caption start near 1640 px. YouTube Shorts showed the problem on Leyte (No. 007): the wordmark, date, and pull line were cut off on a phone but looked fine on a computer. The numbers were measured from Brian's YouTube and TikTok screenshots on an iPhone.
* `renderer/card.html` (VSAFE) keeps all text 11% in from each side, starts the header below TikTok's top bar, ends any text beside the buttons at 76% of the width, and keeps nothing below about 1600 px. The footage or photo window runs edge to edge, so a trimmed side never cuts a border.
* Living Cards and Story Cards share this frame (`vFrame`). Story Card captions hold six lines; `renderer/story.js` stops with an error if a caption needs more, so shorten it.
* Check a new layout with a simulated phone view (the frame cropped to 9:19.5 with the app overlays shaded) before posting.

### The hook line (Living Cards)
Under the pull line, every vertical Living Card carries one short typewriter line, the **hook**, stored as `video.hook` (or `video_b.hook` on a second cut) in the dispatch JSON. It fills the space above the app captions and gives a viewer with the sound off a reason to keep watching.
* **About 45 characters**, two lines at most. Model: "He promised in 1942 to come back. Here he is." (Leyte).
* It must **add something the pull line doesn't**: the stakes, the before, or the payoff. Never restate the pull line or the date. A second cut's hook differs from the noon hook.
* Facts only from the dispatch's `facts` block, like every script. No dashes.
* `renderer/living.js` stops with an error if the hook is missing or runs past two lines. The X and 4:5 videos don't show it.

## Bottom strip (X and 4:5 videos)
Video players draw over the bottom of a video: the timestamp in the bottom left, the mute button in the bottom right (Brian's X and Threads screenshots, 20 and 21 Oct 2026), and on a desktop the play, progress, volume, and speed bar along the bottom with dark shading behind it. So the X and 4:5 Living Card videos get the same extra paper at the bottom of the bottom band as the X still card (Cards doc), and the date, place, pull line, and handle all sit above it, with the pull line and handle at the normal right edge. The top band stays the same so the film window stays as large as possible. If the desktop bar is ever seen touching the place line on a large monitor, add a little more room to the videos only. The vertical videos use the vertical safe zone instead.
* **Taller strip for X and Threads (25 Oct 2026, from Brian's No. 017 screenshots):** the phone mute button on X and Threads reaches about 10% of the card width up from the bottom, and the Threads desktop button about 12.5%, so it covered the handle. The X video now gets a 9 unit strip and Threads gets its own 4:5 file, `dispatch-NNN-threads.mp4`, with a 10.5 unit strip (1 unit is 1% of the card width). Only the bottom edge of the film window moves up, with the date, place, pull line, and handle moving up with it; the layout is otherwise unchanged. The Instagram and Facebook 4:5 file keeps the 5 unit strip, and the vertical video is never touched. The strips live in `STRIP` in `renderer/card.html`.

## Living Cards
The post card with archival footage playing in the photo window. Same bands, same stamp, no added effects, so it reads as the same brand in a feed.
* **Four files from one clip:** X 1600 x 1440, Instagram and Facebook 1080 x 1350, and Threads 1080 x 1350 (all with the bottom strip kept clear, taller on X and Threads); vertical 1080 x 1920 for TikTok and YouTube Shorts (inside the vertical safe zone, with the hook line).
* **Clips** are cut on Thursday from the source film research secured (the `film` block names the file and the stretch that holds the shots). They are short and loopable, about 15 seconds. The first frame doubles as the thumbnail, so it must be a strong shot. **Open on motion or action in the first two seconds** (Test B, from the 23 Oct 2026 run; see the playbook): viewers leave within a few seconds, so start the cut on the strongest moving shot rather than people standing or a still street.
* **Sound:** keep the film's own period sound whenever the source has it, especially battle sounds on Battle Lines. Cut the audio together with the picture so each sound stays with its shot, and normalize loudness to about -16 LUFS. Set `"sound": true` in the dispatch JSON's `video` block so `renderer/living.js` keeps the clip's audio (otherwise it adds a quiet blank track for compatibility, as on silent Signal Corps film). Never use a stretch where music or narration plays under the shots. Note in the YouTube description that the sound is the original soundtrack of the source film. Documentaries in the Film Library carry narration and score almost throughout, so their clips run silent unless the library lists a natural sound stretch and it passes a listen.
* **Stamp:** the normal series stamps. A special video stamp was considered ("Elite" rejected); revisit only if the normal stamps don't feel distinct enough. **On a Flash day** (Research doc, "Flash days") the noon video keeps the normal card frame and bands, and the stamp reads FLASH in ink (`renderer/card.html` handles `"series": "flash"`); the full art treatment belongs to the still card only. Everything else about the video is as usual.
* **Hook:** a `video.hook` line for the vertical video (see the hook rules above).
* **Files:** dispatch-NNN-x.mp4, -instagram.mp4, -threads.mp4, -vertical.mp4 (a second cut adds `-b`: dispatch-NNN-b-x.mp4 and so on). `node renderer/living.js <id> --only=x,threads` renders just those shapes and leaves the others alone; add `--cut=b` for a second cut.

### Film sources
**The Wednesday research run does the film hunt and the downloads (Research doc); this section is the source list it uses. Start with the Film Library doc:** it lists the films already indexed (with NARA cloud copies where they exist), every usable shot by calendar day with exact times, what not to use inside each film, and the leads still to check. Thursday only goes back to these when a secured film fails and a quick replacement exists.
* **U.S. government film:** Army Signal Corps film (NARA Record Group 111, 111-ADC series) and Navy film (428-NPC) are U.S. government works, and many have MP4s on the NARA catalog that download from Claude's workspace. Search the NARA catalog with typeOfMaterials=Moving Images. Signal Corps shot cards (PDFs on each record) describe every shot, which makes finding the right moments fast. Other U.S. government film at NARA is fair game too, including finished films like Tunisian Victory, The True Glory, and The Battle of Russia, which NARA holds reel by reel.
* **Wartime British official film** whose Crown copyright has expired (for example Desert Victory, PD-UKGov on Commons).
* **Captured Japanese film** (added 1 Nov 2026): Japanese newsreels and Navy film released before 1953 under an organization's name are public domain, and NARA holds many as 428-NPC reels. The Japanese Navy's own film of the Pearl Harbor launch (naId 81567) is the model. Check every shot against the 1942 Toho feature film with studio miniatures, which is often passed off as real (Film Library). Captions say it is Japanese film.
* **Universal Newsreel:** believed donated to the public domain; confirm its status at NARA before relying on it.
* **Avoid:** United News reels (NARA flags possible copyright from pooled commercial footage), British Pathé, Movietone, and colorized or remastered versions. **German newsreels (Wochenschau and others) and Italian newsreels anywhere, including inside an Allied documentary** (the Bundesarchiv claims the German rights). Soviet newsreels unless Commons has cleared that specific file; get the Eastern Front through U.S. made films such as The Battle of Russia. Leave out any staged or reconstructed shots and any feature film clips in a documentary; the Film Library lists them by time for every indexed film.
* The Internet Archive is blocked from Claude's workspace.
* **Wikimedia Commons video:** Commons hosts many public domain wartime films, but upload.wikimedia.org refuses large video downloads from Claude's workspace (429, asks for a 10 minute wait, and keeps refusing). What works: Brian opens the file link in his browser, saves it into the WW2 Dispatches\Source Film folder, and Claude pulls it from there in a Project chat with his computer linked (the scheduled runs can't reach it, and the Microsoft 365 connector only sees the ARC work account, not his personal OneDrive). Files must be under 400 MB to transfer, so use Commons' 480p transcode for long films (.../transcoded/<path>/<file>.480p.vp9.webm). Only the cut clip leaves Brian's computer: the Project chat commits it to the videos repo at `inbox/<filename>`, and the next run's sweep moves it to `footage/<filename>` on R2. The full film stays in Source Film as the library. When NARA has the same film, use the NARA reels instead, so no Brian step is needed. **Films over 400 MB even at 480p** (the Stalingrad 1943 transcode is 586 MB; also The True Glory, Tunisian Victory, and The Memphis Belle copies in Source Film) can't be staged from Brian's computer. In a Project chat, cut them straight from Commons instead: the 429 refusals usually clear after a few retries about 45 seconds apart, then ffmpeg reads just the needed stretch over byte ranges (input seek with `-ss` before `-i`, re-encode for frame accuracy), so the whole film never downloads. First check that the Commons file's size matches the Source Film copy byte for byte, so the Film Library times line up. This worked on 14 Nov 2026 for the Uranus shots. **Research files the download task on Wednesday night, due Thursday 5 p.m. Mountain**; Thursday then leaves the slot open and files the cut task (Video count above).
* **Source Film on Brian's computer** holds Desert Victory (480p, the only copy we have; already cut for Nos. 013, 021, 024, 033, and 037), The Battle of Russia (both parts), The Battle of Midway, Tunisian Victory, The True Glory (restored), and The Memphis Belle. All are indexed in the Film Library. Keep the full films; they are the library.
* **Hard to get footage:** the famous Leyte wading ashore shot is on Signal Corps reel 111-ADC-2704 (NARA 16508), not digitized online. NARA's Motion Picture branch (mopix@nara.gov) can make copies for a fee if it's ever worth it. The same goes for Daitoa News #1 (NARA 35995, Japanese newsreel of Pearl Harbor and Hong Kong).

## Story Cards
Archival photos with slow movement, plus typed cards for scenes no camera caught, captions on screen for every line, and an AI narration of a short script.
* **Script:** drawn only from the dispatch's `facts` block (Research doc). **Never add facts.** Stored in `stories/<dispatch-id>.json` as segments (spoken line, caption, visual). Keep each on screen caption to six lines (about 140 characters).
* **Photos:** four to six real archival photos per Story Card, already downloaded by research into `photos/` and listed in `videoPhotos`. If a video turned into a Story Card on Thursday (no usable film), use the photo leads research left in `videoPhotos` and fetch them; NARA still pictures (such as the 208 and 111-SC series) download reliably. Never pad with weak or unrelated photos. If the photos can't be had, use a bank video and say so.
* **Voice: Onyx** (Kokoro TTS voice am_onyx at 0.95 speed, American male), one voice for everything. Kokoro is open source (Apache 2.0), free for commercial use, and runs in Claude's workspace. Setup: download kokoro-v1.0.onnx and voices-v1.0.bin from github.com/thewh1teagle/kokoro-onnx releases (model-files-v1.0) into /home/claude/tts as model.onnx and voices.npz, and pip install kokoro-onnx soundfile. The narration audio is not kept in the repo; `renderer/story_tts.py` regenerates it identically when a Story Card needs re-rendering. Revisit the voice after a month of analytics.
* **Pronunciation (spoken script only):** write years as words ("nineteen forty two"), U-boat numbers as digits spoken ("U five sixty eight"), and spell tricky names phonetically ("Karny" for Kearny, "Shershell" for Cherchell). The on screen captions keep the real spelling.
* **Pronunciation dictionary (`renderer/pron.json`):** for words a respelling can't fix, `story_tts.py` swaps in exact sounds from this file. It holds Montgomery, El Alamein, objectives, and antitank (added 29 Oct 2026 after Brian flagged Nos. 033 and 037). When Brian flags a word, add it here rather than respelling it in one script, so every future Story Card gets it right. Axis names (German, Japanese, and Italian places, ships, and people) will need this often on 2:30 card videos; add each one the first time it comes up.
* **Build:** `renderer/story_tts.py` voices it; `renderer/story.js` renders videos/dispatch-NNN-story-am_onyx.mp4 (1080 x 1920, about 60 to 75 seconds, inside the vertical safe zone) in the card frame. A Story Card render takes 10 to 15 minutes, so run it in the background with a long timeout. Then **watch the finished video's frames** and check the captions match the narration.
* **Models:** No. 011 (Clark's secret landing, the first Story Card) and **No. 015 (UN Charter), the model for Story Card captions and settings**.

## Tagging each video
After a video is finished, add `video` to the dispatch's `themes` block (`video_b` for a second cut): `kind` (Living Card or Story Card), `opening`, and `sound` (period sound, silent, or narration), using only the values in `analytics/themes.md`. **Tag `opening` from the finished video's actual frames at about 0.3 and 2 seconds** (a video that isn't on disk comes from R2: `python3 renderer/media.py get videos/<file>`), never from memory or the source description. The Tuesday analytics review compares hold by opening shot and by slot, so this tag is what measures Test B. The rest of the `themes` block is research's; leave it as is.

## Captions and text for videos
* Videos keep **short captions**; the full story lives in the cards. A Living Card caption points at the film (for example, "Film from Red Beach, 20 October 1944. MacArthur heading ashore on Leyte.") and draws only from the `facts` block. No "film at noon" or "film tonight" line in any card caption. When the camera wasn't American, the caption says whose it was ("Soviet film", "Japanese Navy film").
* **Never repeat another caption (7 Nov 2026), on any platform.** Every video caption (X, Instagram, Threads, Facebook, the TikTok caption, and the YouTube title and description opening) gets its own hook and its own wording. No sentence or distinctive phrase may be copied from that day's 8 a.m., 2:30 p.m., or 5 p.m. caption, the day's other video, or a Name This post; a bare date line like "14 November 1944." is fine. Write the video caption about the film itself: what the viewer sees, whose camera, when it was shot. Before scheduling, compare each video caption against that day's other captions and rewrite any shared phrase of more than a few words. The noon run writes first; the evening run reads the noon captions in the JSON and writes around them, and the Friday cards run writes the card captions around both. Why: on Threads, noon videos that repeated the morning caption (24, 29, and 31 Oct and 6 Nov) averaged under 120 views, and Threads appears to treat a repeated caption as the same post and hold back its reach.
* Each video stands on its own: **no reply links it to a card post**, and no reminder or task is ever made for one.
* **Hashtags:** X and Facebook put #OTD first; video posts with their own tag set (Story Cards, Facebook Reels) also open with #OTD. Threads has no hashtag (History Threads topic). Anniversary event tags follow the Cards doc. Video captions never carry the Declass line.
* **No shared wording with the Campaign Series.** On days a Campaign Series post runs at 6:30 a.m. (Campaign Series doc), a video on the same event is fine, but its captions and titles never reuse a sentence or hook from `campaign-series/<series>/day-NN.json` for that date (`body`, `threads`, `subtitle`). The series is written first.
* **Story Cards:** every caption says "Narration is AI generated". The YouTube description lists the photo credits.
* **YouTube Shorts:** a short, hook first title and a description with the story, sources, and a channel line. **No support line until the support page launches** (Project Brief, Support); at launch it becomes "Support WW2 Dispatches: ww2dispatches.com/support" at the end. Never a Ko-fi line. Category Education. Two Shorts a day need two different titles.
* **TikTok:** a shorter, punchier caption with a few hashtags.

## Posting in Buffer
Every noon video posts at **12:00 p.m. America/Denver**, and every 7 p.m. video at **7:00 p.m. America/Denver**, on all six channels (channel IDs in the Brief). The time comes from the dispatch's `videoTime` ("12:00" when it is missing; "19:00" for the evening video; a second cut is always 19:00).
* **X:** the X video.
* **Instagram:** a Reel, shouldShareToFeed true. The still stays in the feed separately.
* **Threads:** the Threads video (dispatch-NNN-threads.mp4), metadata {threads: {type: "post", topic: "History Threads"}}.
* **Facebook:** the 4:5 video, type **"post" for Living Cards** (as Nos. 009 and 013 were) and **"reel" for Story Cards** (as Nos. 011 and 015 were).
* **YouTube Shorts:** the vertical video, with a title and categoryId "27" (Education).
* **TikTok:** the vertical video, with a title.
* Story Cards have one vertical file, used on all six channels.
* **AI flags:** Story Cards are flagged AI generated wherever Buffer offers it (X, Instagram, TikTok, YouTube isAiGenerated true). Living Cards carry no AI flag.
* **Swapping a file in a scheduled post:** Buffer re-checks the whole post on edit, so send the new video link together with the post's current metadata (YouTube title and category, TikTok title, Instagram type and share to feed, Facebook type, Threads topic, AI flags). A re-rendered file never replaces the old one: `renderer/media.py` uploads it under a new name (dispatch-NNN-x-r2.mp4 and so on), so give Buffer the new link the renderer printed. Nothing can serve a stale copy. **Instagram edits must resend the video asset too**, even for a caption only change, or Buffer rejects the edit.
* **Record the Buffer ids** in the dispatch JSON: for Story Cards a `story` block with the buffer ids and a `kit` sub block {caption, youtubeTitle, tiktokCaption, schedule, thumbnail}; for Living Cards the film keys (`x_film`, `instagram_reel`, `threads_film`, `facebook_film`, `youtube`, `tiktok`) under `buffer`, a `kit` block, and kits/dispatch-NNN-kit.md; for a second cut the `_b` keys, `kit_b`, and kits/dispatch-NNN-b-kit.md (Evening video). The Friday cards run adds the card keys beside them. The analytics match each video post by these ids, so when a post is reposted, record the new id in place of the old one. When a Story Card is replaced by a Living Card, its `story` block moves to `replacedStory`.

## Where videos live
* Every video, poster, trimmed clip, and source film lives in the Cloudflare R2 bucket served at **https://media.ww2dispatches.com**, under `videos/` and `footage/` (for example https://media.ww2dispatches.com/videos/dispatch-057-x.mp4). Never in ww2dispatches-cards. **Don't commit videos to ww2dispatches/ww2dispatches-videos by hand**: since 6 Nov 2026 it is only the inbox below (and media.py's fallback target). The cards repo ignores .mp4 files in videos/ and footage/, node_modules/, and stories/audio/.
* **Credentials:** the runs read R2_ACCESS_KEY_ID, R2_SECRET_ACCESS_KEY, R2_ENDPOINT, R2_BUCKET, and R2_PUBLIC_URL from the environment. Never print, echo, or log their values. Uploads need boto3 (`pip install boto3` if it is missing).
* **Every run starts with the media sweep:** `python3 renderer/media.py sweep`, before anything else. It clones the videos repo beside the cards repo (only `inbox/` is checked out), uploads each file in `inbox/` to R2 as `footage/<filename>` (never overwriting: an existing name gets the next free `-rN` name), checks it at the public link, then removes it from `inbox/` with git rm and pushes. It also uploads anything in the videos repo's `videos/` or `footage/` that R2 lacks (fallback commits, Project chat renders) and points `media-keys.json` at R2. Commit and push `media-keys.json` if it changed, and put any PROBLEM line in the summary.
* **The inbox:** the ww2dispatches-videos repo stays live (never archive it) as the inbox for clips made where R2 can't be reached, mainly a Project chat cutting a `needs brian` film from Source Film. That chat commits the clip to `inbox/<filename>` (the filename the dispatch's `video.footage` will use, for example `inbox/1944-11-20-leyte.mp4` becomes `footage/1944-11-20-leyte.mp4`), nothing else in the repo. Old Buffer posts keep their GitHub links, which keep working.
* **Fallback, never silent:** if any R2 variable is missing, every media.py command prints a block of WARNING lines, and media.py commits videos, posters, and footage to the videos repo the old way instead (same paths, never overwriting, `-rN` for a changed file), records the GitHub link in `media-keys.json`, and prints that link for Buffer. **The run's summary must report the WARNING to Brian.** The next run with credentials moves those files to R2 in its sweep.
* **Never overwrite:** `renderer/media.py` is the only way files go to R2. It never replaces a file. Uploading the same bytes again does nothing; a re-render of an existing name goes up as the next free `-r2`, `-r3`, ... name (dispatch-057-x-r2.mp4), and `media-keys.json` (repo root) records which copy is current, so the site and the Declass catalog link the newest one. Commit and push `media-keys.json` whenever it changes (the cards repo).
* **Steps:** `node renderer/living.js <id>` (add `--cut=b` for a second cut) fetches the footage clip from R2 if it is not already in footage/, renders, and uploads each video and poster, printing the public link of each; `node renderer/story.js <id>` does the same for the Story Card video. Make the Instagram poster frame and upload it with `python3 renderer/media.py put videos/dispatch-NNN-instagram-poster.jpg` (the same for a new trimmed clip in footage/). Confirm each link returns the file, then give Buffer exactly the link printed (https://media.ww2dispatches.com/videos/<file>). No `?v=` is needed for videos. `python3 renderer/media.py url <path>` prints the current link of any file.
* **Two runs, one repo:** the noon and evening runs both push to the cards repo on Thursday night. Each pulls (`git pull --rebase`) before it commits, and each touches only its own fields: the noon run the 8 a.m. dispatches' `video`, `kit`, `story`, `themes.video`, and film keys; the evening run the 19:00 cards' same fields and the second cuts' `_b` fields. If a push is refused, pull, rebase, and push again; never force push.
* **One time cleanup:** the cards repo still has old .mp4 files committed in videos/ and footage/. No unsent post uses a cards repo video link any more (No. 011 moved to the videos repo on 20 Oct 2026), so the next run can remove those .mp4 files with git rm (copies are in the ww2dispatches-videos repo), commit and push, note it in the summary, and remove the item from `status.md`. Never rewrite history or force push.
* GitHub doesn't let Claude's sessions create Releases, which is why the separate repo exists.

## Checks and review by exception
* **Platform checks:** Buffer's per channel daily limits, a YouTube title and category on every Short, TikTok and YouTube AI content labels, and "Narration is AI generated" in each Story Card caption. Report any posting limit hit.
* **Caption check:** no video caption shares a sentence or distinctive phrase with that day's other posts (Captions section).
* **Second cut check:** no shot in a second cut also appears in the noon clip, and its opening frame differs.
* **Review by exception:** the Schedule Board flags only what needs a human eye: any fact with fewer than two sources, a photo whose public domain status is unconfirmed, any quote, and anything changed after the source check.

## Watch items and risks
These inform decisions but do not block the daily pace.
* The Saturday review time (about 45 minutes), any factual corrections, the video bank size, the real Claude usage per week, and how many photos and films Brian has to supply.
* A factual slip in a narrated script or a hook line. Neither ever adds facts beyond the `facts` block.
* Weak photo videos on filler days. Never pad; use a bank video instead.
* AI narration labelling and the pronunciation rules.
* Run length and Claude usage with fourteen videos a week, split over two Thursday runs so neither runs long. Review the real figures after the first week on the new schedule.
* Repetition. Narrated photo videos can feel samey, and second cuts can feel like reruns; picking for film first should keep Story Cards rare, and the Tuesday analytics review watches hold and reach by video kind, opening, and slot (noon against 7 p.m., second cuts against first cuts) and proposes a change if they drop.
* Reach per post with five posts a day: the Tuesday analytics review reports reach per post and total daily reach per platform against the four weeks before the change; Brian decides any trim (Analytics doc).
