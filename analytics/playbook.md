# WW2 Dispatches Playbook

The current lessons from the Tuesday analytics review. The Wednesday research run, the Thursday video run, and the Friday cards run read this before picking events, building videos, and writing hooks and captions. It holds lessons, not rules: the Project docs stay the rules, and anything here that would change a rule goes to Brian first as a Schedule Board task. The Tuesday review rewrites this file each week; older versions live in git and in `analytics/reviews/`.

Updated Tue 27 Oct 2026, after the first real review (`analytics/reviews/2026-10-27.md`). The method is in the Project's Analytics doc.

## How to read this
* **Reach:** a post's views compared with a typical post of the same kind on the same platform (1.0 is typical).
* **Response:** weighted reactions per 100 views (like 1, comment 3, repost, quote, share, or save 2), compared the same way.
* Cards, Name This, and videos are each judged only against their own kind.
* **Hunch:** seen in one review, or fewer than 5 posts. The runs may lean toward it but never treat it as settled.
* **Pattern:** holds across 3 weekly reviews with 5 or more posts on each side. The runs follow it.
* **Rule change:** a pattern that would change a Project doc. Filed for Brian to approve.

## For the Wednesday research run (events and subjects)
Hunches, all from one review:
1. **Famous faces and famous voices lift reach.** Cards centered on leaders averaged Reach 2.1 over 6 posts; Tojo's portrait (4.1) and Churchill's broadcast (3.5) led all cards. *Lean:* when a date offers a well known person at a real moment, it is a strong pick, especially for an evening Photo of the Day or Quote of the Day.
2. **Barrier breaking stories about groups often left out of WW2 coverage get strong reactions.** The WAVES card (the Navy's first Black women officers) drew Response 5.5, the highest of any card. Still one post. *Lean:* when a date offers a genuine story of this kind (the Tuskegee Airmen, the 6888th, Navajo Code Talkers, the Nisei 442nd, the WASPs, women in war work), it is a strong pick on its merits. Choose it for the story, never as a token, and keep the usual verification and somber subject rules.
3. **Quiet procedural events reach fewer people.** The tin can drive (0.26), the Nuremberg indictment (0.46), and the UN Charter (0.55) were the weakest cards. *Lean:* favor the event with a vivid moment or person; save procedural events for days with nothing stronger.
4. **Turning points and firsts do well.** Aachen (the first German city to fall), MacArthur's return, and Mark Clark's secret landing all reached about 1.8 to 2.5. *Lean:* a clear "first" or turning point is a good 8 a.m. pick.
5. **For videos, the film matters more than how it opens.** The best Living Cards (Aachen, Alamein) had clear, famous film of a big moment. *Lean:* when choosing between events for the noon video, pick the one with the strongest real film; a Living Card beat a Story Card on every platform so far (Reach 2.2 against 0.68, 3 and 2 posts).

## For the Thursday video run (openings, sound, kinds)
Hunches:
1. **Story Cards are the weak spot.** Both narrated Story Cards (No. 011 and No. 015) ran over a minute, opened on a still, and drew 36 and 18 YouTube views (typical 537), with 6 to 11 percent hold on Instagram. *Lean:* keep Story Cards as the fallback only; Test C (proposed, not approved) would try shorter ones.
2. **A dark opening did not hurt.** The Alamein Living Card opens nearly black and held best of all (45 percent on TikTok, 38 on Instagram). The Leyte card, opening on people standing still, held worst (15 to 16 percent). *Lean:* an opening with something happening, even at night, beats a still group.
3. **YouTube Shorts is the biggest video platform so far** (Living Cards 800 to 1,600 views each). TikTok second (300 to 700).
* Keep tagging the real opening from the finished video's frames for Test B.

## For the Friday cards run (hooks, formats, captions, questions)
Hunches:
1. **People and faces lead evening photos.** Portrait cards averaged Reach 3.8 (2 posts), groups 1.3 (4), machines 1.1 (5). *Lean:* for a Photo of the Day, prefer a strong photo of a person or people.
2. **Quote of the Day can be the best post of the week** when the line is famous and hopeful (Churchill: 90 reactions, 6 reposts). One post. *Lean:* pick quotes people will want to share.
3. **Spec Sheets that open on the machine draw less.** The three before Test A averaged Reach 0.54. The first Test A post (No. 018 Johnston, opening on the 327 men and 141 survivors) drew 1,102 Threads views in two days. *Run:* follow Test A.
4. **5 p.m. cards ran ahead of 8 a.m. cards** (Reach 1.56 against 1.25, Response 1.75 against 1.01, 8 and 9 posts). First reading only; nothing changes.
* Engagement questions: only one card with a question has posted (No. 020, too early). Compare once there are 5 with and 5 without.
