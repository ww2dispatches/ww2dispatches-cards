## How to read this
* **Reach:** a post's views compared with a typical post of the same kind on the same platform (1.0 is typical).
* **Response:** weighted reactions per 100 views (like 1, comment 3, repost, quote, share, or save 2), compared the same way.
* Cards, Name This, and videos are each judged only against their own kind.
* **Hunch:** seen in one review, or fewer than 5 posts. The runs may lean toward it but never treat it as settled.
* **Pattern:** holds across 3 weekly reviews with 5 or more posts on each side. The runs follow it.
* **Rule change:** a pattern that would change a Project doc. Filed for Brian to approve.
* "Review 2 of 3" means a finding has held with enough posts in two weekly reviews; one more and it becomes a pattern.

## For the Wednesday research run (events and subjects)
1. **Leaders at a real moment lift reach** (review 2 of 3). Cards centered on leaders: Reach 1.99 over 9 posts. Greece says no (3.16), Tojo (4.34), and Churchill (3.69) are among the best cards. *Lean:* when a date offers a well known leader at a decisive moment, it is a strong pick for either slot.
2. **Battle Lines and Statecraft out reach Blueprint and Home Front** (review 2 of 3). 2.01 and 2.20 against 0.81 and 0.67 on Threads. *Lean:* give the 8 a.m. slot to combat, turning points, and big decisions; keep Blueprint mostly for evening Spec Sheets.
3. **Home Front and everyday subjects reach fewer people, but those who see them react** (review 1 of 3 with enough posts). Civilians 0.51 over 5, home front theater 0.66 over 8, with Response 1.26 to 1.9. The tin can drive, the draft lottery, and the UN Charter were among the weakest. *Lean:* pick a home front story when it has a vivid person or a striking moment (WAVES, This Is the Army), not a procedure. Never pick two procedural subjects for the same day (the draft lottery story and Spec Sheet on 29 Oct both reached about 0.6).
4. **Turning points and combat do well** (review 1 of 3 with enough posts). Turning point 2.10 over 6 posts, combat 1.81 over 12. *Lean:* a clear first, a breakout, or a stand against the odds is a strong 8 a.m. pick.
5. **Stories of groups often left out of WW2 coverage draw the strongest feeling** (hunch, 2 posts). The WAVES card led Response in week one (5.12); the Japanese American rescue of the Lost Battalion was the best card of week two (Reach 3.87, Response 3.01). *Lean:* when a date offers a genuine story of this kind (the Tuskegee Airmen, the 6888th, the Navajo Code Talkers, the 442nd, the WASPs), it is a strong pick on its merits. Choose it for the story, never as a token, with the usual verification and somber subject rules.
6. **For videos, the film decides the reach.** Living Cards drew a median 1,418 YouTube views against 149 for Story Cards, and held about twice as long. The best videos (Aachen, Rommel's counterattack, Lost Battalion, Snipe, Supercharge) all had clear film of fighting or a big moment. Brian's own read (27 Oct): action scenes do much better. *Lean:* when choosing between events for a day, the one with strong usable combat or action film usually wins. Desert Victory and Navy combat film have served well.

## For the Thursday video run (openings, sound, kinds)
1. **Open on something happening** (Test B, closed with a positive result; keep doing it until Brian decides). Action first openings held an average of 43 percent on TikTok against 32 before; Instagram stayed at 27. Keep tagging the real opening from the finished video's frames at about 0.3 and 2 seconds.
2. **Story Cards are the weak spot** (hunch, 4 posts, the widest gap in the data). YouTube 22 to 259 views against about 1,400 for Living Cards; Instagram hold 6 to 15 percent. *Run:* follow Test C below for every Story Card, and make Story Cards only when no usable film exists.
3. **Period sound and silent film both work.** Period sound 1.55 over 3 posts, silent 1.58 over 5. No reason to prefer one; keep the film's own sound when it has it. No. 039 (Manila Bay) is the one video with a music bed (Brian's exception of 28 Oct), tagged sound "music" so it is measured on its own.
4. **YouTube Shorts is the biggest video platform** (Living Cards about 1,000 to 1,700 views each), then TikTok (300 to 1,100). Facebook Reels draw about 280 when they show, but 5 of 10 Living Card Reels drew almost nothing (see Watch).
5. **A dark opening did not hurt** (Alamein, 45 percent on TikTok), but set the Instagram and TikTok cover to the strongest frame, as the Videos doc says.

## For the Friday cards run (hooks, formats, captions, questions)
1. **Hooks that name the human price or the odds work best.** "To reach 211 trapped Texans, a unit of Japanese American soldiers paid with hundreds of casualties" (3.87), "Nineteen small antitank guns, dug into a desert hollow in the dark" (3.18, 15 comments), "Of the 327 men aboard USS Johnston, only 141 came home" (2.54). *Lean:* open on people, a number that hurts, or a stand against the odds.
2. **Spec Sheets: open on the people** (Test A, closed: Response doubled, Reach not clearly better; keep doing it until Brian decides). Spec Sheets still reach less than other formats (0.75 over 7 posts, against 1.68 for Story and 1.76 for Photo of the Day). *Lean:* keep the human first hook, and favor Spec Sheets whose machine has a human story inside it (Johnston, the Spruce Goose) over a first flight or a statistic.
3. **Faces lead evening photos** (hunch, 4 posts). Portraits averaged Reach 2.94. *Lean:* for a Photo of the Day or Quote of the Day, prefer a strong photo of a person.
4. **Quote of the Day works when the line is famous and hopeful.** Churchill (3.69) far beat FDR's "The shooting has started" (0.57). Two posts. *Lean:* choose a line people already half know or will want to share.
5. **Engagement questions:** three cards so far (Reach 0.34), all weak subjects, one held back on Threads. Too few and too tangled to judge. Keep following the Cards doc.
6. **Slots:** 8 a.m. led this week (1.73 against 1.29) after 5 p.m. led last week. No lesson; nothing changes.
