## How to read this
* **Reach:** a post's views compared with a typical post of the same kind on the same platform (1.0 is typical).
* **Response:** weighted reactions per 100 views (like 1, comment 3, repost, quote, share, or save 2), compared the same way.
* Cards, Name This, and videos are each judged only against their own kind.
* **Hunch:** seen in one review, or fewer than 5 posts. The runs may lean toward it but never treat it as settled.
* **Pattern:** holds across 3 weekly reviews with 5 or more posts on each side. The runs follow it.
* **Rule change:** a pattern that would change a Project doc. Filed for Brian to approve.
* "Review 2 of 3" means a finding has held with enough posts in two weekly reviews; one more and it becomes a pattern.
* **Read this week's card numbers with care.** Threads, the only card platform that counts, cut the account's reach by about 80 percent from 5 Nov (see Watch). Cards posted 5 to 9 Nov score low for reasons outside the card.

## Patterns
1. **Battle Lines cards out reach Blueprint and Home Front cards** (pattern since 10 Nov 2026, three reviews). Battle Lines 2.47 (median 2.20) over 16 posts, Blueprint 1.31 (median 0.93) over 11, Home Front 0.73 (median 0.67) over 7. *Follow:* see research item 1.

## For the Wednesday research run (events and subjects)
1. **Give the 8 a.m. slot to Battle Lines when the date allows** (pattern). Combat, a turning point, or a stand against the odds, with usable film, is the strongest 8 a.m. pick. Blueprint and Home Front fit better in the evening slots or as a Spec Sheet, unless the story has a vivid person or moment.
2. **Combat and turning points do well** (review 2 of 3). Combat 2.30 over 18 posts, turning point 2.38 over 10. Rommel's retreat from Alamein (8.36, the best card ever) came at the end of a week of Alamein posts: following a famous campaign across several days seems to build an audience for its climax. *Lean:* when a big campaign runs over several dates, follow it to its turning point.
3. **Home front and everyday subjects reach fewer people, but those who see them react** (review 2 of 3). Home front theater 1.02 (median 0.70) over 14, civilians 0.91 over 9, everyday life 0.57 over 6, with Response 1.1 to 1.3. *Lean:* pick a home front story when it has a vivid person or a striking moment (WAVES, This Is the Army, the Fu-Go schoolgirls), not a procedure. Never pick two procedural subjects for the same day.
4. **A famous leader at a dramatic moment does well; leaders at procedure do not** (hunch, restarted 10 Nov). Tojo takes power (4.97), Churchill to France (4.23), Greece says no (3.62) are among the best cards, but the leader group's median is only 0.73, pulled down by elections and speeches (FDR's third and fourth terms, both very low, though the second fell into the Threads drop). *Lean:* a leader at a decision under pressure, yes; an election result or a routine speech, only with a strong human angle, and never two in the same week.
5. **A human story inside a big machine or strike lifts the card** (hunch). Manila Bay through Admiral Shima watching from shore (3.47), the Fu-Go balloon through the schoolgirls who glued it (2.91), the Spruce Goose through Hughes's dare (2.95). *Lean:* when picking a machine or air strike, check the facts for a person whose moment can carry the hook.
6. **Stories of groups often left out of WW2 coverage draw the strongest feeling** (hunch, 3 posts). WAVES (Response 4.38), the Lost Battalion (Reach 4.47, Response 2.58); the Navajo code talkers card was held back by Threads (29 views) and never got a fair test. *Lean:* when a date offers a genuine story of this kind, it is a strong pick on its merits. Choose it for the story, never as a token, with the usual verification and somber subject rules.
7. **For videos, the film decides the reach.** Living Cards: YouTube median 1,286 against 256 for Story Cards; TikTok hold 44 against 19 percent. Supercharge drew 2,610 YouTube views, the most yet. *Lean:* when choosing between events for a day, the one with strong usable combat or action film usually wins. Desert Victory and Navy combat film keep serving well; short newsreel of crowds can hold too (Roosevelt's fourth term, 75 percent on TikTok).
8. **Same day pairs:** the Fu-Go Story and Spec Sheet both did well (1.75 and 2.91), the draft lottery pair both did poorly. A strong subject carries two posts; a weak one sinks both.

## For the Thursday video runs (openings, sound, kinds, second cuts)
1. **Story Cards are the weak spot** (review 1 of 3 with 5 posts each side). Reach 0.80 against 1.31; Instagram hold 12 against 30 percent; TikTok 19 against 44. *Run:* follow Test C below for every Story Card, and make one only when no usable film exists.
2. **Openings: combat or firing slightly ahead of motion** (hunch, 6 posts each). Reach 1.31 against 1.03. Not settled; keep following the Videos doc, and tag the real opening from the finished video's frames at about 0.3 and 2 seconds.
3. **Silent film and period sound do equally well** (silent 1.34 over 9, period sound 1.32 over 5). Keep the film's own sound when it has it. No. 039 (Manila Bay) is still the only video with a music bed, tagged "music".
4. **Short and plain can hold.** No. 043 (Roosevelt's fourth term, silent newsreel) held 75 percent on TikTok, and Torch 63 percent on Instagram, the best holds yet. A short film with one clear thing happening keeps viewers.
5. **YouTube Shorts is the biggest video platform** (Living Cards typically about 1,300 views), then TikTok (about 480). Instagram Reels steady at about 125. Facebook Reels draw about 270 to 300 when they show but often vanish (see Watch).
6. **Second cuts and 7 p.m. videos** start 15 Nov. No data yet; each is scored as its own post, and the review compares 7 p.m. with noon and second cuts with first cuts once each has 5 posts.
7. Brian dropped Test B as a standing practice on 4 Nov; the playbook no longer adds anything to the Videos doc on openings.

## For the Friday cards run (hooks, formats, captions, questions)
1. **Hooks that name a person, a price, or a dare work best.** "Hitler's order was victory or death. Rommel chose neither." (8.36), "To reach 211 trapped Texans, a unit of Japanese American soldiers paid with hundreds of casualties" (4.47), "Admiral Shima was ashore at a staff conference when the planes came" (3.47), "Howard Hughes told the Senate that if his giant plane failed, he would probably leave the country" (2.95, 12 comments). *Lean:* open on one person's choice or a number that hurts, in one or two short sentences.
2. **Spec Sheets reach less than Story cards** (review 2 of 3 by median: 0.67 against 1.86), but the best ones (Spruce Goose, Fu-Go, Johnston) rival the stories. Brian dropped Test A as a standing practice on 4 Nov, so the hook is the cards run's call under the Cards doc; the numbers still favor a Spec Sheet whose machine has a human story inside it.
3. **Faces lead the evening photos** (first reading with 6 posts). Portraits 2.36 (median 2.14); place or scene photos 0.50 over 4 and people in action 0.67 over 5 trail. *Lean:* for a Photo of the Day or Quote of the Day, prefer a strong photo of a person.
4. **Quote of the Day works when the line is famous** (hunch, 4 posts, median 0.39). Churchill to France (4.23) far beat FDR's lines; Hitler's "few small pockets" fell into the Threads drop. *Lean:* choose a line people already half know or will want to share.
5. **Engagement questions:** 7 cards, median 0.27, average 1.82 (one big winner). Too mixed to judge. Keep following the Cards doc.
6. **Slots:** 8 a.m. led again (2.09 against 1.29), the second review running. Not a pattern yet; nothing changes. The 2:30 p.m. Axis cards start 15 Nov and are compared once they have 5 posts.
