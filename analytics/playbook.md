The current lessons from the Wednesday analytics review. The Thursday cards run and the Friday video run read this before picking events, writing hooks, and building videos. It holds lessons, not rules: the Project docs stay the rules, and anything here that would change a rule goes to Brian first as a Schedule Board task. The Wednesday review rewrites this file each week; older versions live in git and in `analytics/reviews/`.

Updated 21 Oct 2026, before the first real review (28 Oct 2026). The method is in the Project's Analytics doc.

## How to read this
* **Reach:** a post's views compared with a typical post of the same kind on the same platform (1.0 is typical).
* **Response:** weighted reactions per 100 views (like 1, comment 3, repost, quote, share, or save 2), compared the same way.
* Cards, Name This, and videos are each judged only against their own kind.
* **Hunch:** seen in one review, or fewer than 5 posts. The runs may lean toward it but never treat it as settled.
* **Pattern:** holds across 3 weekly reviews with 5 or more posts on each side. The runs follow it.
* **Rule change:** a pattern that would change a Project doc. Filed for Brian to approve.

## Hunches
These came from a test run on launch week data (17 to 21 Oct 2026), before the system was live. They still need real data.
1. **People and faces lead evening photos.** The evening photos of people drew more than the objects and machines. *Runs:* when choosing a Photo of the Day, prefer a strong photo of a person or people.
2. **Spec Sheets drew less on Threads.** Spec Sheets stay at two a week (Brian's call); Test A tries a better hook.
3. **Barrier breaking stories about groups often left out of WW2 coverage get strong reactions.** The WAVES dispatch (the Navy's first Black women officers) had by far the best response. Brian's read: the subject drove it, more than the hook. *Runs:* when a date offers a genuine story of this kind (the Tuskegee Airmen, the 6888th, Navajo Code Talkers, the Nisei 442nd, the WASPs, women in war work), it is a strong pick on its merits. Choose it for the story, never as a token, and keep the usual verification and somber subject rules.
4. **Quiet procedural events reach fewer people.** The indictment filing and the tin can order drew least. *Runs:* when a date offers a choice, favor the event with a vivid moment or person; save procedural events for days with nothing stronger.
5. **Video hold depends on the opening shot.** Viewers left Living Cards within a few seconds. *Runs:* see Test B.
