## Summary
The second week looks a lot like the first, which is the first sign of something real. On Threads the cards that won were big, human moments of combat or defiance: the Japanese American rescue of the Lost Battalion (1,698 views, 88 reactions), the guns at Outpost Snipe (1,391 views, 15 comments), and Greece saying no to Mussolini (1,383). Living Cards keep pulling far ahead of Story Cards: a typical Living Card drew 1,418 YouTube views against 149 for a Story Card, and held viewers about twice as long on Instagram and TikTok. ## Tests
* **Test A, human first Spec Sheet hooks: closed, promising but not proven.** Every Spec Sheet since 25 Oct opened on people (Johnston's 327 men, the 6,175 men who held the first draft number, the 400 Rushmore carvers, Howard Hughes before the Senate, the schoolgirls who built the Fu-Go balloons). Scored test posts (018, 020, 026, 030) against the three before (002, 008, 014): average Reach 0.88 against 0.57, but the median fell slightly (0.41 against 0.49), because one big winner (Johnston 2.54) carried the average and Rushmore was held back on Threads. Response doubled (1.23 against 0.59). Early signs from the Spruce Goose (731 Threads views in a day) are good. Read: the human first hook draws more reactions and costs nothing; it does not by itself make a Spec Sheet reach like a story. The subject still matters most. Recommendation: keep it as the standing practice (decision filed for Brian).
* **Test B, action first Living Card openings: closed, it helped on TikTok.** Test videos scored so far (017, 019, 021, 024, 027) held an average of 43 percent on TikTok against 32 for the three before (007, 009, 013), and 27 percent on Instagram against 27. Early numbers from Supercharge (66 percent on TikTok, 32 on Instagram) point the same way. Reach did not change with the opening; the film and the subject drive that. Read: an opening with something happening keeps TikTok viewers; Instagram is flat. Recommendation: keep it as the standing practice (decision filed for Brian).
* **Test C, shorter Story Cards: now running.** Brian approved it on the Scoreboard on 28 Oct, but last week's playbook still listed it as proposed, so no Story Card has been built under it yet (No. 041 Odenwald, Fri 6 Nov, was built before). It starts with the 5 Nov video run. Brian's note asks for Story Cards that are more intense, which is what the test tries: under 30 seconds, opening on the most dramatic still. Review moved to 24 Nov so at least two test Story Cards can be measured.
